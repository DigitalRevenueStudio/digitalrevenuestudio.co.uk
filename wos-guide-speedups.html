Speed-Ups:
Where They Score
and When to Save

What speedups do, where they score points and when to save them, with the details we could not confirm marked clearly.

Rebuilt 29 October 2026. Earlier versions of this guide gave Hall of Chiefs days that do not match the event, said a wrong-type speedup is consumed with no effect, set an 8 hour rule with no source and called a 5 minute timer a waste of 55 minutes. We could not support those points. This version shows where speedups score, what guides recommend, and where we could not confirm a detail.

⚡ Quick Summary
Types
Construction, research, training and general
Best Windows
SvS prep days, plus Hall of Chiefs Day 7 in Generation 2
Core Rule
Save them for scoring windows and long timers
Biggest Mistake
Spending a large stock before a scoring day
Types

Know What You Have

🏗️
Construction

Speeds up building upgrades, including long Furnace upgrades. This is the most common type.

🔬
Research

Speeds up research timers.

⚔️
Training

Speeds up troop training and promotion.

⚡
General

Guides describe these as usable on any timer, which makes them the most flexible type. Check an item's description to confirm what it applies to.

One guide also lists expert skill learning speedups. We found no source on what happens if you choose a speedup that does not match the timer, and we could not confirm that it is consumed with no effect, as an earlier version said. Read the item screen before you confirm.

Event Value

Where Speedups Score

A speedup finishes the timer and, in some events, also earns points. The guides we read agree on only a few places.

Earlier versions of this guide said Hall of Chiefs Day 1 scores construction speedups, Day 3 scores training and Day 4 scores research. That does not match the schedule in our Hall of Chiefs guide, where construction and research score on the power they add, so we removed it.

The Rules

Speedup Rules for F2P Players

  • Use them on long timers. Short timers finish by themselves, so a speedup is better kept for a long upgrade or a scoring day. No guide gives a cut-off, so choose one that suits your account.
  • Check the amount. We could not confirm whether the game consumes an item whole or only the time needed. Look at the amount shown before you confirm, and try a small case first.
  • Plan around scoring days. If SvS prep or Hall of Chiefs is close, hold the types that score instead of clearing queues.
  • Start saving early. Guides range from one to four weeks. One says at least three weeks for free-to-play players. Another says to stop casual use two weeks before the event. Choose a window you can sustain.
  • Do not stall your account. A delay of weeks costs more than it earns. Keep your main upgrades moving.
  • Use long upgrades well. One guide reports that a Furnace 30 upgrade can take more than 40 days, and says general and construction speedups suit it. Research that reduces construction time, such as Tool Improvement and Construction Technology, is also advised.
Community Feedback

What Players Report

These are player opinions, not guaranteed best practice.

  • Hoarding pays off in competitive events. Players say those who rank highest in event scoring are often the ones who saved the longest. We have not seen a source that measures this.
  • Keep some general speedups back. Players like holding general speedups as a buffer for a type they run short of on an event day.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Clearing the queue before an event. You arrive with fewer speedups than you could have scored.

Assuming every event scores speedups. Only a few do, and the type and day vary.

Using General speedups on short timers. They are the most flexible type, so keep them for when you need them.

Copying a day plan from another event. Check the event screen before you spend.

Waiting too long. Holding speedups for weeks while your Furnace sits idle can cost more than the points are worth.

Check on your own screens. Which speedup types score on each day of your event, the points per minute, whether an item is consumed whole, and how much time the screen says you will use. The numbers here come from published guides, not from the game.
