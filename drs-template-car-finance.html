Free Template: Car Finance

Car Finance DCA Complaint Letter

Based on the letter used by Digital Revenue Studio in July 2024, updated to reflect the 2025 court rulings. Free to use, no sign-up required.

What has happened since: the final scheme, the legal challenge and what it means for you (updated 18 November 2026)

Important note on this template

When I originally wrote to my lenders in July 2024, my letter only asked about discretionary commission arrangements (DCAs). At that point, the wider commission types had not yet been tested in court.

Following the Court of Appeal ruling in October 2024 and subsequent FCA announcements, the scope of the review expanded. This template has been updated to reflect those developments and now covers all three commission types relevant to the FCA review.

Already wrote in 2024? If you wrote asking only about DCAs and received written confirmation, your complaint is still valid. You do not need to write again.

How to use this template

  1. Check your agreement falls before 28 January 2021, agreements after this date are outside the FCA review window.
  2. Find your lender, check old finance documents or your credit file if you are unsure.
  3. Replace every highlighted placeholder with your own details.
  4. Send directly to the lender by email, keep a copy with timestamps.
  5. You do not need a claims company, doing this yourself means you keep 100% of any redress.
  6. Note the date sent and keep any acknowledgement you receive.
Changed since: updated 18 November 2026The final scheme covers agreements taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024, not only those before 28 January 2021. That date is when the FCA banned discretionary commission, so later agreements can still be in scope through high commission or a contractual tie. Read what has happened since ↓

Important: not all DCAs result in compensation

This is something most guides do not make clear enough. Having a DCA confirmed on your agreement does not automatically mean you are entitled to a refund. The type of DCA matters significantly.

There are three types:

Negative DCA, the broker or dealer used the arrangement to increase your interest rate in order to earn a higher commission. This is the type most likely to result in redress. This is what all three of my lenders confirmed in my case.

Neutral DCA, a DCA was present on the agreement but was not used to change your rate. Your interest rate was not affected. People in this situation are unlikely to receive compensation as they were not financially disadvantaged.

Positive DCA: the arrangement was used to lower your interest rate. While this may have reduced the broker's commission, you benefited from a better rate. No compensation is expected in this scenario.

The letter below asks your lender to confirm whether any commission arrangement was present. Once confirmed, the nature of that arrangement, and whether it disadvantaged you, will determine whether you are eligible for redress under the FCA scheme. No official figures for compensation amounts have been confirmed. The FCA redress scheme has not been finalised and until it is, no one can say with certainty what individual payouts will look like.

There are exceptions, for example where the commission was very small or no interest was charged, so not every agreement will lead to a payment. Car Finance DCA Complaint Letter

[Your Full Name]
[Your Address Line 1]
[Your Address Line 2]
[Your Town/City]
[Your Postcode]
[Your Email Address]
[Date]

Customer Services / Complaints Team
[Lender Name]
[Lender Address]

Dear Sir or Madam,

Re: Motor Finance Agreement, Request for Information and Formal Complaint

I am writing to you regarding a motor finance agreement I held with you. To help you locate my account, my details are as follows:

Full name:[Your full name]
Date of birth:[DD/MM/YYYY]
Current address:[As above]
Address at time of agreement:[If different from above]
Finance agreement number:[Check original paperwork or credit file]
Vehicle registration:[Number plate]
Approximate date of agreement:[Month and Year]
Dealership or broker used:[Name if known]

I am writing to ask a straightforward question: was any form of commission arrangement attached to my agreement?

Specifically, I would like to know whether my agreement involved any of the following:

A discretionary commission arrangement (DCA), where the broker or dealer had the ability to set or adjust the interest rate on my agreement and received a higher commission the higher the rate they set.

A high commission arrangement, where the commission paid was equal to or greater than 35% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan amount.

A contractual tie, where an arrangement between you and the broker provided exclusivity or a right of first refusal.

If I was not made aware of any such arrangement at the time, I do not believe it was adequately disclosed to me. This is likely to have been unfair and may have prevented me from negotiating the terms of my agreement or seeking a better deal elsewhere.

If any of the above arrangements were present in connection with my agreement, please treat this letter as a formal complaint. I would ask that you respond within 8 weeks in line with FCA guidelines.

Please confirm receipt of this letter and confirm whether you are treating it as a complaint.

Yours sincerely,

[Your Full Name]
[Your Signature: if sending by post]

Changed since: updated 18 November 2026The final rules set the high commission test at 39% of the total cost of credit and 10% of the loan. The letter asks about 35% or more, the figure proposed when it was written, so it still covers the final test and works as written. Read what has happened since ↓

What to expect after sending

The FCA has paused the requirement for lenders to respond to complaints while a redress scheme is being finalised. If you receive a holding response this is completely normal, it does not mean your complaint has been rejected.

If the lender confirms a commission arrangement was attached, ask them to specify the type. A negative DCA, where your rate was increased for higher commission, is what the FCA review is primarily concerned with. Keep any written confirmation safe as it is important evidence.

No official compensation figures have been confirmed. Any figures you may have seen in the media are speculation based on early estimates. The FCA consultation is expected to conclude around end of May 2026, after which the redress scheme will be clarified.

For further background, MoneySavingExpert.com is a comprehensive resource. Martin Lewis has covered this topic in detail.

What has happened since

Updated 18 November 2026

The guidance above is left as it was written, so you can see how this has moved on. This section covers what has changed and what it means if you have already complained, or are thinking about it.

Where my own complaints stand: every lender I wrote to has logged the complaint. None has made an offer yet, because the redress scheme has been held up by the legal challenge described below.

The timeline so far

  • October 2024The Court of Appeal ruled on three car finance cases, widening the issue beyond discretionary commission.
  • 1 August 2025The Supreme Court overturned most of that ruling, but agreed that one customer had been treated unfairly under the Consumer Credit Act because of the size of the commission and how it was hidden.
  • August to October 2025The FCA confirmed it would set up an industry-wide redress scheme and published its proposals for consultation.
  • March 2026The FCA published its final rules for the Motor Finance Consumer Redress Scheme.
  • 30 June and 31 August 2026Lenders’ preparation periods ended, first for agreements from April 2014 onwards and then for older agreements.
  • July 2026The FCA partly suspended the scheme after it was challenged at the Upper Tribunal.
  • 3 November 2026The Tribunal hearing was moved to 16 February to 3 March 2027, and the FCA set out what it expects from any settlement offers made in the meantime.
  • 18 November 2026Lenders say people who complained by 30 June 2026 about an agreement from April 2014 onwards should hear by now if they are not owed anything. For older agreements complained about by 31 August 2026, the date given is 18 January 2027.

Who the final scheme covers

  • Car finance agreements taken out between 6 April 2007 and 1 November 2024.
  • Hire purchase, personal contract purchase (PCP) and conditional sale. Leasing (personal contract hire) is not included.
  • It runs in two parts: agreements before April 2014, and agreements from April 2014 onwards, each with its own dates.

What counts as unfair

An agreement is treated as unfair by default if one of these was not properly disclosed to you:

  • A discretionary commission arrangement, where the dealer or broker could set your interest rate and earned more the higher it was.
  • High commission, of at least 39% of the total cost of credit and at least 10% of the loan.
  • A contractual tie that gave a lender exclusivity or first refusal with the dealer.

There are exceptions. Agreements where the commission was very small (£120 or less before April 2014, £150 or less after) or where no interest was charged are generally excluded.

How much you might get

Redress is worked out with a set formula, with interest added on top. The FCA estimates that around 12.1 million agreements could be eligible, with an average payment of about £830 per agreement. That is an average, so your own figure depends on your agreement, the commission and what you paid.

What the legal challenge means

The scheme has been challenged at the Upper Tribunal by several finance companies and a consumer group. While the challenge is heard:

  • Lenders do not have to work out or pay redress under the scheme.
  • Lenders are still expected to tell people who complained if they are not owed anything.
  • If the scheme is upheld and not appealed, the FCA expects payments to start in 2027. If it is overturned, a revised scheme could push compensation back to 2028 or later.

If your lender makes you an offer now

Some lenders may offer to settle before the challenge ends. The FCA has said any offer made during this period should be at least the most you could get under the scheme, and you should get at least a month to decide. If you turn an offer down, you cannot take it to the Financial Ombudsman while the challenge is running. This is general information rather than advice, so read any offer carefully before you accept it.

If you get a “not owed” letter

Keep it with your other paperwork and check the reason against the points above: the date of your agreement, the type of finance and the kind of commission. If the reason is not clear, ask the lender to explain it in writing. Once the scheme is running, the Financial Ombudsman can check whether a lender followed the scheme rules.

If you have not complained yet

Under the scheme, lenders are meant to contact customers with a relevant arrangement and invite them to opt in, with the original timetable ending on 31 December 2026 for agreements from April 2014 onwards and 28 February 2027 for older ones, and six months to respond. The suspension may change these timings, so check your lender’s website. Writing to your lender with the letter above is still free.

Do you need a claims firm?

No. The scheme is free to use, and the FCA says you do not need a claims firm or a law firm. Those firms can take up to 36% of any compensation, including VAT.
