Pets:
Upgrade Order, Capture Chain and Refinement

The 14 pets, what their skills do, how capture requirements and advancement gates work, and a practical way to plan levelling, advancement and refinement.

Rebuilt 29 October 2026. Earlier versions of this guide gave one fixed unlock-day table, told players to take Hyena, Wolf and Tapir to the maximum first, ranked the pets in a single tier list and said to put all refinement into one pet and spend marks only in SvS prep. The published information we checked does not support those as rules, so this version separates the decisions, shows the documented numbers and marks where players disagree.

⚡ Quick Summary
Core Rule
Treat levelling, advancement and refinement as three separate budgets
Early Route
Meet capture requirements and develop the skills you actually use
Refinement
Build broadly first, then specialise with care
Biggest Mistake
Following one fixed list of which pets to max, in which order
Overview

Three Separate Decisions

"Upgrade my pets" is really three different purchases, each with its own materials. A pet can be worth levelling for its skill, a low priority for further advancement and still worth some inexpensive refinement. A pet with an unimportant skill can still add useful secondary attributes.

SystemMain materialsWhat it develops
LevellingPet FoodPet level and the basic troop bonuses that come with it
AdvancementTaming Manuals, plus Energizing Potions and Strengthening Serum at later gatesProgress through each milestone and skill development
RefinementCommon Wild Marks and Advanced Wild MarksSecondary combat attributes

Keep a separate budget and a separate priority for each one. The pet you level first does not have to be the pet you refine first.

Access

Pets Unlock Over Time

Pets need Furnace Level 18 and a state that has reached the relevant release point. Later pets also have capture requirements that involve earlier pets. These are three separate conditions, and meeting one does not meet the others.

We have removed the unlock-day table that earlier versions carried. The published day tables we found contradict each other, in one case placing a prerequisite pet later than the pet that needs it, so a single schedule is not safe to copy. Follow the capture screen for your state when a published timeline differs.

Skills

Active Skills Are Not the Whole Pet

Each pet profile lists three separate things: basic troop bonuses, refinement potential and an active skill. Pressing the skill button activates the skill only, not every attribute on the pet. Skills from different pets can be active at the same time, so you do not have to choose just one combat pet.

Running together does not mean working everywhere. Some battle modes allow pet bonuses but switch off deployment capacity effects, so check the mode before you rely on a skill.

These are the published skill ranges across skill development. They are not predictions of battle results, and the top of each range is not what every owner receives, so read the current level on your own pet.

Capture Chain

Capture Requirements Shape Your Spending

Later pets need earlier pets at specified levels. The documented chain runs: Arctic Wolf before Musk Ox, Musk Ox before Giant Tapir, Giant Tapir before Titan Roc, Titan Roc before Giant Elk, Giant Elk before Snow Leopard, Snow Leopard before Cave Lion, Cave Lion before Snow Ape, Snow Ape before Iron Rhino and Iron Rhino before Saber-tooth Tiger, with further requirements for the later pets. The level needed at each step is shown on the capture screen.

That is why "ignore every early pet and save for the newest one" can block the capture you are saving for. Before a large spend, separate three things: the minimum development needed to capture the next pet, the development that gives you a useful skill, and any extra development that only adds stats.

Advancement

Advancement Gates and What They Cost

Pet Food raises the level. Every ten levels there is an advancement gate with its own materials, and these are separate purchases from the food.

MilestoneMaterialsWhat changes
Level 10Taming ManualsThe first advancement payment
Level 20Taming ManualsA larger manual requirement
Level 30Taming Manuals and Energizing PotionsPotions become a second bottleneck
Level 40Taming Manuals and Energizing PotionsBoth costs rise
Level 50Taming Manuals, Energizing Potions and Strengthening SerumSerum enters the cost
Later milestonesThe same three materialsCosts keep rising where the pet supports those levels

Costs differ between pets, so a cost needs the pet's name as well as the level. These are payments at each gate, not running totals, and they do not include the Pet Food needed to reach the level. They come from published pet reference tables, so check them against your own screen before you plan around them.

Advancement atCave HyenaSnow Ape
Level 1015 Manuals35 Manuals
Level 3045 Manuals, 10 Potions110 Manuals, 15 Potions

Level 100Not listed730 Manuals, 135 Potions, 100 Serum

Do not stretch a cheaper pet's costs across to a later pet. The published tables also end at different levels for different pets, so a missing figure does not mean a free advancement.

  • Reached and advanced are different. A pet at Level 50 with its advancement still pending has not paid the Serum or received the advancement benefits. "Take every pet to 50" is unclear unless it says which of the two you mean.
  • Food is a separate bill. The food listed for a milestone only covers getting into that level. Count the food for every level you cross, plus every unfinished advancement on the way.
  • Advancement is not worthless to skip. Stopping before the Serum gate saves materials, but advancement also gives a bigger basic stat step. For example, Snow Ape at Level 50 is listed at roughly 13.7 percent basic troop Attack and Defence before advancement and roughly 15.4 percent after.

Before you pay for a gate, compare what it improves, how often you would use that skill, whether it unlocks another capture and what else the same materials could fund. Level 50 is the clearest point to pause and make that comparison on purpose.

Early Pets

Use the Skills You Will Actually Use

Ownership, regular use and further advancement are three different questions, and a skill can be worth having without its next upgrade being your best purchase.

  • Cave Hyena. Activate it before you start a construction you care about, and compare the displayed timer first. Do not assume it works the same on a timer that is already running, and do not delay an important upgrade for a long time just to wait for the next window.
  • Arctic Wolf. It restores Chief Stamina, so use it when you will spend that stamina on something that helps your progress. It is not a Bear Hunt feature.
  • Musk Ox. It completes gathering on the next eligible wilderness resource tile, not every march, and Secured Alliance Gathering Nodes are excluded. Prepare a suitable march, check its load against the tile and avoid using it on a nearly empty node.
  • Giant Tapir. It makes Pet Food, so it can speed later development when food is what is holding you back. Work out the payback on the extra food, which is the extra food spent reaching the upgrade divided by the extra food collected per day. That ignores the advancement materials, so it cannot prove the upgrade is best overall.
  • Giant Elk. It finds an item. Read the reward information on your own screen and do not count on a particular scarce item every day.

Refinement

How Refinement Works

Refinement proposes changes to a pet's secondary stats, and the changes only take effect when you select Replace. A proposal can lower a stat as well as raise one. The warning prompt appears when Total Power would drop, not for every individual drop, so a roll can raise your displayed power while lowering a stat you care about. If you are choosing for a particular stat, read the individual changes instead of relying on the absence of a warning.

A pet's own rarity and the colour its refined attributes reach are different things, and they are often confused. The documented protection is narrow: once a secondary stat's quality has increased, a later refinement does not lower that quality because the bonus dropped. That does not mean individual percentages lock in, that a maxed stat can never change or that every accepted roll pays off.

Refinement Route

A Broad-First Refinement Route

Refinement and levelling can point at different pets. For a general free-to-play account, our default is:

  • Obtain the pets available to you. Neglected pets benefit most from cheap early refinement.
  • Compare your least developed refinement bands. Spread early marks before you pile into one pet.
  • Use Common Wild Marks while they still give worthwhile gains. Hold Advanced Wild Marks for when ordinary rolls become consistently poor.
  • Review, do not assume. Blue and purple are commonly suggested points to reassess, not official thresholds.

A later pet can have a higher refinement ceiling, but a higher ceiling does not by itself mean the next mark gives more benefit. Judge a pet by the gain relevant to your account for each mark spent.

ApproachBenefitLimitation
Accept increases in total powerSimple, and progress stays broadYou can accept a drop in a stat you care about
Accept only selected statsFits a specific roleYou may sacrifice other useful stats and use more rolls

Our default is to build broad attributes first and to target narrowly only when you have a clear objective and understand what you are giving up. We found no support for the idea that the other attributes "do not matter".

Auto-refinement is convenient, but it is not proof of the best result. Read its acceptance conditions, set a spending limit, check what it protects and review the outcome before continuing. Reports differ on whether manual choice improves long-term results.

We have not located a dependable official table of refinement success rates or mark costs, so this guide does not quote one. Do not rely on a fixed success rate, a guaranteed number of marks per colour or the idea that repeated bad rolls guarantee a good one.

Events

Saving Materials for Events

Published SvS preparation rules include pet advancement and refinement as scoring actions, and players often recommend saving those actions for the event. Event tasks change, and some events can ask for pet levelling too, so a rule like "food never scores" is too broad. See our SvS Prep guide and read the current stage wording.

  • Separate feeding, advancement and refinement. Each one can score differently.
  • Save scarce materials when waiting is reasonable. Do not hold a useful recurring skill back for weeks just to follow a saving rule.
  • Check the scoring condition. Do not assume that spending points requires a successful roll.

Before a relevant fight, check that the mode allows the effect, activate the skills you plan to use, confirm the buffs are active and check your deployment capacity before you send. Pet skill bonuses can appear among the special bonuses on a battle report.

A sustainable routine is simple: use Arctic Wolf when the stamina will be useful, collect Giant Tapir's food, use Giant Elk, pair Musk Ox with a suitable gathering action, line Cave Hyena up with worthwhile construction, and save combat skills for real activity. There is no need to log in whenever a cooldown ends.

Community Feedback

What Players Report

These are player experiences, not guaranteed best practice. Players disagree on several points, so compare the reasoning with your own account.

  • Growth pets first is common, but not unanimous. Many players favour Hyena, Wolf, Tapir and other regularly used pets early. Others go straight for combat development. Players also disagree about how far to take growth pets before the costs climb.
  • Broad refinement has strong support. Players describe taking several pets through the early bands before going deep on one, and some report better short-term results. These are observations, not a measured roll table.
  • Newest pets only is a competing view. Some players favour later pets for their potential. That is about eventual ceiling, not gain per mark.
  • Targeted refinement is disputed. Some players prioritise stats such as Infantry Health or offensive Lethality and use advanced marks later. Others object that narrow targeting gives up too much overall progress.
  • Stop at 50 is often vague. Players mention pausing at the first Serum gate but often do not say whether they mean reached or advanced.
  • Gems for marks is an opportunity cost. Some players buy Common Wild Marks with spare gems, especially when it helps an event task. Others keep gems for hero plans. Compare what you would delay.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Following one fixed priority list. The route is a series of decisions: capture access, skills you use, role-based advancement, then refinement.

Treating food, advancement materials and marks as one pool. They do different jobs and need separate plans.

Ignoring the capture chain. Skipping an early pet can block the one you are saving for.

Saying "Level 50" without saying advanced or not. The two states cost different amounts and give different benefits.

Assuming the skill button activates everything. Basic bonuses and refinement attributes are separate from the active skill.

Confusing rarity with refinement quality. They are different things.

Hoarding for weeks. Compare the recurring benefit you lose by waiting with the event reward you would gain.

Check on your own screens. The capture screen for your state, the current advancement cost for your next gate, what the next advancement improves, whether a capture accepts reached or advanced levels, whether a pet's effect works in the event you play, and the scoring wording of the current event. Published tables can contain errors, so confirm any figure before you plan around it.
