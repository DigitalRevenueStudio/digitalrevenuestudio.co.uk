FAQ Websites & SEO

SEO for Small Business Websites: Your Questions Answered

What SEO is, what a new site needs to be found on Google, how to earn backlinks the right way and what to expect. Checked against Google’s own guidance.

SEO Basics

What is SEO?

SEO (search engine optimisation) is the work of helping search engines find, understand and show your pages to the people looking for them. The visits it brings are free, unlike paid ads, and they keep coming as long as the page stays useful.

The work falls into three areas:

  • On-page SEO: the content itself, clear titles, headings, meta descriptions and answering what the searcher actually wants.
  • Technical SEO: making the site easy to crawl and index, with fast pages, a good mobile layout, HTTPS and a clean sitemap.
  • Off-page SEO: your reputation beyond your own site, mostly links from other websites (backlinks), covered in detail below.
Are there different types of SEO?

Yes, depending on the business. Local SEO helps a business appear in map results and local searches, usually through a Google Business Profile. E-commerce SEO focuses on product and category pages. More recently, people talk about optimising for AI answers: writing clear, well-structured, trustworthy content that search engines and AI tools can understand and cite. The foundations are the same for all of them.

Do I still need SEO if I run paid ads?

Usually, yes. Ads stop bringing visitors the moment you stop paying, while a page that ranks keeps working. Many small businesses use ads to test what people respond to and SEO to build visibility that lasts.

How is AI changing search?

Search results now often include AI-written summaries, so simple questions can be answered without anyone clicking through. The pages that still earn clicks tend to offer something a summary cannot: first-hand experience, original information, worked examples and a clear point of view. That is why everything in our Knowledge Hub is written from things we have built, used or checked.

Building the Site

Do I need to pay for hosting?

Not at launch. We build static, fast sites on free hosting and move to paid options only when a business needs more. A static site is quick to load, secure by default and cheap to run.

Why does mobile-first matter?

Most visitors arrive on a phone, and Google uses the mobile version of a page for indexing and ranking. A site that looks good on a laptop but is awkward on a phone will lose both visitors and rankings.

What is a canonical tag?

A line in the page that tells search engines which URL is the main version of the page. It stops duplicate versions of the same page competing with each other.

Content and Keywords

How do I choose the right keywords?

Start with the words your customers actually use, not the ones you use internally. Look at what people type into search, the suggestions search engines offer and the questions customers ask you. Then pick phrases that match what you offer and that you can realistically compete for. Search intent matters more than volume: a smaller phrase from people ready to buy is worth more than a big one from people just browsing.

How often should I publish new content?

Consistency matters more than volume. In our experience a few genuinely useful pages a month beat a daily stream of thin posts. Keeping existing pages accurate counts too, which is why our guides show when they were last checked.

What is a meta description, and does it affect rankings?

It is the short summary that can appear under your page title in search results. Google’s SEO Starter Guide recommends one that is short, unique to the page and covers its most relevant points. It is not a ranking factor in itself, but a clear description can persuade more people to click.

Why do FAQ pages help?

They answer real questions in plain language, which is how many people search. Each FAQ page gives Google a clear topic to match, and links to related pages that spread that relevance across the site.

Getting Found on Google

What is a sitemap and do I need one?

A sitemap is a file listing the pages you want search engines to find. Yes, you need one. It helps Google discover new pages and understand how your site fits together.

What is Google Search Console?

A free Google tool that shows how your site appears in search, which pages are indexed and what problems Google found. Submitting your sitemap there is one of the first steps after launch.

What are breadcrumbs for?

Breadcrumbs show where a page sits on your site, for example Home, then FAQ, then a topic. They help visitors move around and help search engines understand your structure. We add them with structured data so Google can show them in results.

Backlinks: Getting Them Right

Backlinks are one of the most talked-about parts of SEO, and one of the easiest to get wrong. This section covers what they are, how to earn good ones and the mistakes that can do real damage.

What are backlinks?

A backlink is a link on another website that points to yours. If a local news site, a supplier or a respected blog links to one of your pages, that is a backlink.

Why do backlinks matter?

Search engines treat a link from another site as a sign that your page is worth visiting, a bit like a recommendation. Links also help search engines discover your pages in the first place. As Google’s SEO Starter Guide puts it, other sites linking to you is something that happens naturally over time when you publish things worth linking to.

What makes a good backlink?

Not all links are equal. The ones worth having usually tick most of these boxes:

  • Relevant: the linking site is about something related to your business or topic.
  • Trusted: it is a real site with real readers, not a page that exists only to hold links.
  • Editorial: someone chose to link to you because your page helps their readers.
  • In context: the link sits naturally in the text, with wording that describes your page.
  • Sending visitors: a good link brings people who click it, not just search value.

One link from a relevant, trusted site is worth more than dozens from sites nobody reads.

How do I get backlinks the right way?

The safest backlinks are earned, not built. The approaches that work:

  • Make something worth linking to: a free tool, a genuinely useful guide, a checklist, original figures or a clear answer to a common question.
  • Join the conversation where your audience is: forums, communities and social platforms, by being helpful rather than dropping links.
  • Use the relationships you already have: suppliers, partners, clients, local organisations and trade bodies often list or feature the businesses they work with.
  • Get featured: local press, podcasts, interviews and round-ups look for useful stories and expert comment.
  • Write for others, carefully: a guest article on a relevant site can work if it is genuinely useful to their readers, not a vehicle for links.
  • Fix missing credit: if someone mentions your business or uses your work without linking, a polite request for a link often works.

Our own experience shows how this works. The Whiteout Survival wiki grew out of a single helpful post in a player community, and players have shared it with their alliances since. Nobody was asked for a link; the guides earned the attention.

What do nofollow, sponsored and ugc mean?

They are labels a site can add to a link to tell search engines what kind of link it is. Google asks sites to mark paid or advertising links as sponsored, links in comments and forum posts as ugc (user-generated content), and to use nofollow when neither applies. Labelled links are not a problem. They can still send you visitors, and a natural mix of link types is normal.

How do I see who links to my site?

Google Search Console has a free Links report showing the sites that link to you most, which of your pages they link to and the link text they use. It is the best place to start before paying for any backlink tool.

What are the pitfalls?

Most backlink problems come from trying to shortcut the process. Google’s spam policies list practices that count as link spam, including:

  • Buying or selling links for ranking purposes
  • Excessive link swaps (“link to me and I’ll link to you”)
  • Automated programs or services that create links for you
  • Low-quality directory and bookmarking sites
  • Keyword-stuffed links hidden in widgets, footers or templates shared across many sites
  • Forum comments with optimised links in the post or signature

Be wary of anyone offering “hundreds of high authority backlinks” for a small fee. Those links are almost always from networks built for exactly the practices above.

What happens if you get it wrong?

At best, the links are ignored and the money is wasted. At worst, Google can apply a manual action, which can push pages or the whole site down in results until the problem is fixed. If you are left with a lot of spammy links, the first step is to get as many removed as you can. Google’s disavow tool is only meant for sites with a considerable number of spammy links that have caused, or are likely to cause, a manual action. In most cases Google can work out which links to trust without it.

Should I pay for backlinks?

Not for rankings. Paying for an advert, a sponsored post or a directory listing is fine if the link is labelled as sponsored, because you are paying for the visitors and the exposure. Paying for links that are meant to pass ranking value is against Google’s spam policies and is the quickest way into trouble.

What to Expect

How long does SEO take?

Longer than most people expect. Google’s SEO Starter Guide says some changes can take effect in a few hours and others take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change. For a brand new site it is usually months before search brings steady visitors, because the site has to be discovered, crawled and trusted first.

That matches our own experience. This site launched in early 2026, and search visits built gradually through the year as more useful pages were published and kept up to date.

Can you promise a first page ranking?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. We can set up the foundations properly and publish content that deserves to rank. Results depend on competition, topic and time.

The short version

Get the foundations right: a fast mobile-first site, a sitemap, canonical tags, Search Console and genuinely useful content. Earn backlinks by making things worth linking to, never by buying them. Then be patient.
