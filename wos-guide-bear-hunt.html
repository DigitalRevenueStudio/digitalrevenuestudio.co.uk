Event Guide

Bear Hunt:
The Complete F2P Guide

Bear Hunt is a 30-minute alliance event where your damage to the bear earns Essence Stones and Hero Gear XP. This guide explains how the event works, how leading and joining differ, which heroes make the best joiners at every generation, and how to get more from your troops.

What Bear Hunt Is

The Event Explained

🐻
How It Works

Bear Hunt centres on your alliance's hunting trap. For about 30 minutes, members launch and join rallies against the bear, and your personal reward depends on the damage you deal. The bear is not trying to wipe out your army, so you are chasing damage points rather than a kill.

⏰
When You Can Join

Your own sessions are spaced two natural server days apart, counted from the 00:00 UTC reset. If you take part on a Monday, you can take part again from Wednesday's reset. A second trap helps with scheduling but does not give you an extra personal session. Follow the live countdown on your event screen, especially if you switch alliance.

💰
Rewards

Bear Hunt gives Essence Stones and Hero Gear Enhancement XP, which support your Hero Gear. Higher damage reaches better reward tiers, and there are one-off Hunting Achievements on top. The 20 October 2026 update raised the top damage reward ranges and added personal damage reward tiers and further achievement milestones, so older screenshots may be out of date.

🚫
No Stamina Needed

Launching or joining a rally uses troops, not Chief Stamina. What limits you is your march count, your troop numbers and how quickly your alliance runs rallies.

Starting vs Joining

Two Roles, Two Different Setups

Leading and joining are scored differently, so the heroes that suit each role are different too.

🎯 Starting a Rally
All three deployed heroes contribute their expedition skills
You supply the rally's combat stats, so Attack, Lethality and your gear matter
Choose heroes by what their expedition skills actually do, not by power or rarity
Your setup sets the ceiling for everyone who joins you
Stagger rally starts with your officers so marches are not all waiting at once
👥 Joining a Rally
Only the first expedition skill of your leftmost hero is offered to the rally
Up to four joiner skills are used. Your other two heroes add no skill, but they can still help you deploy more troops
Your troops still decide your own damage score: numbers, tier and ratio all matter
Pick the leftmost hero by that one skill. A strong hero with a defensive first skill is a poor choice here
Keep a preset ready so you can join in a few taps

How the four joiner skills are chosen: skill level decides, not hero rarity or power. A higher-level skill replaces a lower-level one, and if two are equal the one that joined first generally keeps its place. A Level 5 skill is hard to displace, but it only helps if it is the right kind of skill, because a Level 5 defensive skill can sit in a slot an offensive skill should have. Before a rally leaves, open the rally details and check the four selected skills. Do not assume the first four names on the list are the ones in use.

Dependable First-Slot Joiners

These are the accessible early options. Figures are the maximum, at Level 5 of the first expedition skill.

Identical flat damage bonuses add together, so four +25% damage skills give about +100% damage dealt. They do not multiply. Mixing Attack and damage dealt may do better because they are different categories, but treat any simple formula as a rough guide and judge by the rally screen.

Defensive heroes make poor joiners. Many strong heroes, including Sergey, Molly, Natalia, Hector and Bahiti, have a defensive or utility first expedition skill. They are valuable elsewhere, but in the first slot they add nothing to the bear's damage. Developing them is not wasted. Just do not put them first when you join.

Key Rules

What Separates Good Scores From Great Ones

  • Check the selected skills, not just who joined. The rally screen shows the four skills in use. If the wrong ones are selected, the leader should correct the lineup before departure.
  • Take your joiner skill to Level 5, then keep that hero in a preset. A maxed useful skill holds its slot against lower-level skills, and a preset saves you scrambling when a rally opens.
  • Use extra heroes to help deploy troops. Your second and third heroes do not add joiner skills, but they can raise how many troops you send. If a small troop cap is already covered by one hero, extra heroes add little.
  • Troops still matter. A joiner contributes troops as well as a skill, and those troops decide their own score. The leader supplies the combat stats the rally uses.
  • Do not expect a stronger joiner to lift everyone else. A stronger account improves its own result, and a good skill helps the whole rally, but swapping a weaker account for a stronger one does not directly raise other people's damage. Use troop caps to manage who gets a place, not because weaker accounts drag the leader down.
  • Position leaders near the trap and use presets. Shorter travel time means more useful activity in a 30-minute window.
  • Turn up when you can. Bear Hunt supports your Hero Gear, so regular sessions pay off over weeks. Agree a schedule your alliance can keep rather than treating every session as compulsory.
Troop Ratios

Which Ratio Should You Use?

Bear Hunt favours marksmen, with lancers usually adding more than infantry. No single ratio suits every account, so treat these as starting points and test them. Ratios are infantry / lancers / marksmen.

RatioWhere it fitsNotes
10 / 30 / 60Early generations and smaller armiesA sensible beginner starting point.
10 / 10 / 80Most accounts with a healthy marksman countThe most common marksman-heavy baseline.
5 / 5 / 90 or higherWell-developed accountsPlayers with large armies report testing more extreme ratios. Test before you adopt one.

How to test: compare two similar rallies with the same leader, the same troop count and tier, and the same selected joiner skills. Change one thing at a time, and look at several results rather than one good hit. Some players keep a minimum amount of infantry and lancers rather than going all in, but we do not have a reliable figure for that, so we do not give one.

Our suggestion for F2P accounts: if you are short of marksmen, think about the whole session. A decent mix in every march usually beats one perfect march while the rest sit idle. Judge your total points across the session, not just your best single hit.

Cyrille

Your Bear Hunt Expert: Cyrille

Cyrille has five Bear Hunt skills, and they do different jobs. Some pay out directly to you, some help the person launching the rally, and one raises only your own damage points.

SkillEffect at maximum (approximate)Who benefits
Weapon MasterFive extra Essence StonesYou, when you claim your reward
ScavengingFive extra x100 Enhancement XP ComponentsYou, when you claim your reward
EntrapmentAbout +300,000 rally capacityThe account launching the rally
Ursa's BaneAbout +30,000 Bear Hunt deployment capacityThe account deploying the troops
Hunter's HeartUp to about +30% Bear Hunt damage pointsYou only (passive)

Hunter's Heart raises your personal damage points. It does not make everyone in the rally hit harder. Entrapment helps the person launching, and Ursa's Bane is deployment capacity, not total rally capacity.

F2P order: Weapon Master and Scavenging first, because they pay out every session. Then Ursa's Bane once you can fill the extra capacity, Entrapment if you lead rallies, and Hunter's Heart as your relationship level grows. You do not need to max Cyrille before other Experts. See the Experts guide for the full order.

Community Feedback

What Players Are Reporting

These are player reports, not tested rules. We include them so you can compare experiences, and we would like to hear from you if you see something different.

  • Mia as a joiner. Her first expedition skill is chance-based, with around a 50% chance of extra damage taken by the target. Some players include one Mia in a rally, while others prefer flat bonuses for consistency. Reports on several copies stacking are mixed, so we suggest at most one and checking the rally screen.
  • Hospital space and casualties. A few players have reported injuries or needing hospital space around Bear Hunt, but most find the bear does not injure their troops. If you see casualties, check they did not come from another battle running at the same time, and send us the battle report.
  • Different bears and newer generations. Some players report that bear targets or newer-generation bears behave differently for older heroes. We have not been able to confirm a penalty, and many players keep using developed older heroes. Replace a hero when the new one performs better for you.
  • Leading with very few troops. Some experienced players send only a handful of troops in their own rally and use the rest to join stronger ones. It only works if enough strong rallies are available, so treat it as an advanced experiment.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Putting a defensive hero in the first slot. Only your leftmost hero's first expedition skill counts when you join. Sergey, Molly and Hector are excellent heroes, but their first skills do not add damage.

Assuming a Level 5 skill is automatically good. A maxed defensive skill can hold a buff slot that an offensive skill should have. Maxing a skill is only useful if it is the right skill.

Not checking the selected skills before a rally leaves. The first four names on the list are not always the four skills in use. Open the rally details and look.

Using the wrong setup for your role. Leading and joining need different heroes. Know which role you are playing before every session.

Putting all your marksmen in one march. A perfect ratio in one march can leave the rest of your marches idle and lower your total for the session.

Mixing up Cyrille's skills. Hunter's Heart is personal damage points only, Entrapment helps the rally launcher, and Ursa's Bane is deployment capacity. Develop the ones that match how you play.

Missing sessions regularly. Bear Hunt rewards Essence Stones and Hero Gear XP, so skipped sessions mean slower Hero Gear progress. Agree a schedule your alliance can keep and attend when you can.
