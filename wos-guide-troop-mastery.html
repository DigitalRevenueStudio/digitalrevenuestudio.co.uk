Troop Mastery:
Types, Tiers and How to Plan Your Army

Infantry, Lancers and Marksmen, how the counter triangle really works, where each tier comes from, and how to plan an army that fits your heroes and your activity.

Overview

The Three Troop Types

Every army is built from Infantry, Lancers and Marksmen. Each type has its own training camp, its own research bonuses and its own heroes, so most of your planning comes down to which type you are going to lean on and why.

🛡️
Infantry

The front line. Infantry take the first contact in a battle, and many players build them first because the early accessible heroes and the frontline role both point that way.

🐎
Lancers

The flexible type. From T7, Lancers have a chance to strike the Marksmen standing behind the Infantry instead of only hitting the front line.

🏹
Marksmen

The ranged damage type. From T7, Marksmen have a chance to strike twice. Many players move towards more Marksmen as their damage heroes and research come online.

⚙️
What Else Matters

Troop tier, Fire Crystal level, research, heroes and numbers all affect a fight. The type is only one of several inputs, so read it alongside the rest of this page.

Counter Triangle

The Counter Triangle Is a Modest Edge

Infantry beat Lancers, Lancers beat Marksmen and Marksmen beat Infantry. The advantage is a bonus, not an automatic win. One source we found puts it at about 10 percent, and others do not give a figure, so treat the size as approximate.

A modest bonus is easy to overrule. A higher tier, a larger army, stronger heroes or better research can outweigh it, so use the triangle as a tie-breaker when two options are close. It is a poor reason to rebuild your whole army. Ask your alliance what it expects to face before a large fight, and see our Bear Hunt guide for how troop choices work in that event.

Stats

What the Troop Stats Mean

You will see several stats on troop and research screens. This is what each one is for, described by role rather than by formula, because we have not found a reliable published combat formula and we do not want to invent one.

StatWhat it is forWorth knowing
AttackHow hard your troops hitResearch and hero bonuses usually apply to a specific troop type
DefenceHow well your troops resist damageMatters most for the troops taking the first hits
HealthHow much damage your troops absorbMore Health means troops last longer in a fight
LethalityAn offensive stat that improves how effectively your troops damage the enemySources explain the exact mechanism differently, so judge it by the bonus shown on your screen
PowerThe account power a troop addsIt is a score for your account, not a battle stat

When you compare two research nodes or two troops, compare what changes on your screen. A larger number on one stat does not tell you how much it changes a real battle.

Tiers

Tiers, Camp Levels and Fire Crystal Levels

An ordinary troop tier is unlocked by the level of the matching troop camp, not by your Furnace level on its own and not by War Academy research. Your Furnace limits how far a camp can be upgraded, so it matters indirectly. The same rule applies separately to your Infantry, Lancer and Marksman camps.

These camp levels are community documented across several troop references. They are not developer confirmed, so check the requirement shown on your own camp screen.

The camps themselves also open at different Furnace levels, so this is not a universal Furnace table and T1 does not become available at the same point for every troop type. These are camp unlock requirements, not the levels at which tiers unlock.

We cross-checked these camp requirements against published building databases, but we have not tested them directly in the game, so confirm them on your own building screen. Upgrading a camp then unlocks stronger tiers as you progress.

Three other progressions sit beside the regular tiers, and they are separate.

Troop skills are reported to unlock at T1, T7, FC3, FC5, FC8 and FC10. We have not seen a complete, reliable description of every skill at each point, so read the skill text on your own troop screen. The T7 steps are the ones we can describe: Lancers can strike Marksmen, and Marksmen can strike twice.

Train, Promote, Deploy

Three Different Actions

These are easy to mix up, so keep them apart when you plan.

  • Train creates new troops at a tier your camp can produce. It uses resources and camp queue time.
  • Promote upgrades existing troops to a higher tier, and unlocking T5 lets you promote lower-tier troops to the highest trainable tier. Compare the cost and time shown on your screen with training the higher tier from scratch before you decide.
  • Deploy is what you actually send out in a march, rally or garrison. Owning troops is not the same as being able to bring them, because march and rally capacity limit how many you can send. Our War Academy guide explains the capacity research.

Some events score training and promotion differently, so read the event scoring rules before you spend a large batch of resources.

Wounded troops go to your Infirmary, and casualties are worked out according to the type of battle. Depending on the fight they can include dead troops, severely injured troops, lightly injured troops and survivors, and a full or empty Infirmary does not change every category the same way. For example, an attack on a player city is documented as killing 35 percent of the attacker's casualties outright, while in Sunfire Castle combat casualties enter the Infirmary until it is full and the rest are treated as deaths. Keeping the Infirmary empty therefore does not mean no troops can die.

When eligible severe injuries exceed the Infirmary's capacity, the Enlistment Office can take some of the overflow as reserves, which you recover using Loyalty. Its documented capacity is four times your Infirmary capacity. It is an extra recovery system, not unlimited hospital space, and we have not seen support for one recovery percentage that applies to every battle. Check the battle report, your Infirmary and your Enlistment Office separately before you work out permanent losses. Event recovery systems may follow different rules, and none of this should be read across to Bear Hunt.

Heroes

Matching Troops to Heroes

Every hero belongs to one troop class. Many expedition skills boost a particular class, so a hero's bonus is only as useful as the troops of that class you bring. Read the skill text for the hero you plan to use before you choose your mix.

Community Feedback

What Players Report

These are player experiences, not guaranteed best practice. Compare the reasoning with your own heroes and goals.

  • Infantry-leaning early. Many players build Infantry first, then move towards more Marksmen as their damage heroes and research develop.
  • Change the mix gradually. Players advise shifting composition as hero investments change, not rebuilding the whole army when a new generation appears.
  • Ask before big fights. Alliance leaders often know what troop types the alliance wants for large rallies, so ask before you commit resources.
  • Check the Infirmary first. Players recommend looking at Infirmary capacity before ordinary PvP, because it limits how many wounded troops can wait for healing. Players also disagree about recovery percentages and sometimes mix up the Enlistment Office with event recovery, so do not rely on a single figure.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Treating the counter triangle as decisive. It is a modest bonus. Tier, numbers, heroes and research can outweigh it.

Copying a fixed ratio. A split that suits someone else's heroes may not suit yours. Start from your heroes and your activity.

Bringing the wrong class for your heroes. If your hero's skills boost one class, bring that class.

Mixing up tiers and FC levels. A new tier comes from your camps or the War Academy. An FC level improves troops within a tier.

Assuming the War Academy is needed for every tier. T1 to T10 come from your camps. The War Academy covers the Helios tier and what follows.

Ignoring capacity. Troops you cannot send do nothing for a rally. Check deployment and rally capacity.

Leaving the Infirmary unchecked. Look at its capacity before ordinary PvP.

Assuming an empty Infirmary means no deaths. Some battles kill troops directly whatever space you have.

Treating the Enlistment Office as unlimited, or as a fixed percentage. It has its own capacity and rules, and not every loss can become a reserve.

Reading a camp table as a Furnace table. The matching camp level unlocks the tier. Your Furnace only limits how far the camp can go.

Check on your own screens. The camp level shown for each tier, the Fire Crystal levels available, the text of each troop skill, the capacity limits for your marches and rallies, and your battle report, Infirmary and Enlistment Office. Exact figures change with updates and differ by account, which is why this guide describes roles and rough scales rather than fixed numbers.
