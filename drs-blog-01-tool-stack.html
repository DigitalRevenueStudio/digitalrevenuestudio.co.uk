The Free Tool Stack We Use to Build Digital Brands

The tools we use across our brands for hosting, email, traffic, publishing and analytics. Free or freemium, used regularly and explained honestly.

6 min readDigital Revenue StudioMarch 2026

Every brand we build at Digital Revenue Studio starts with as few paid tools as possible. That is not because money is no object. It is because we want to know what is achievable without a budget. If something costs money, we say so. Most of what we use is free or freemium, and most of it is very good.

Here is the tool stack we use across the studio’s brands, with what each tool is for and when you might outgrow it.

Websites and hosting

Cloudflare Pages and GitHub: free static hosting

Our websites are static HTML held in a GitHub repository and served by Cloudflare Pages. Hosting is free, deployment is fast and uptime is reliable. For static sites, which covers most of what we build, it is hard to justify paying for hosting until you have a specific reason to.

When to upgrade: static hosting works until you need server-side features such as databases, user accounts or dynamic content. For a blog, portfolio, wiki or brand website it handles everything you need.

IONOS: domain and DNS

Low-cost domain registration and DNS management. Pointing a custom domain at your host takes about ten minutes and a couple of DNS records. The only ongoing cost is the domain registration fee.

Email marketing

Sender: a free plan with automation

Sender’s free plan includes automation sequences and a sign-up form builder, with a generous allowance for subscribers and monthly emails. Check the current limits on their pricing page. We run a separate list for each brand so every audience only gets content relevant to them. Read our full Sender review or the Sender FAQ for the detail.

Content and traffic

Reddit: the community platform

Reddit has been our most useful community platform by a wide margin. A single helpful post can reach hundreds of thousands of people, and the Whiteout Survival wiki grew out of a conversation in r/whiteoutsurvival. The full story is in How a Viral Reddit Post Accidentally Became a Gaming Wiki. The approach is to take part first and post second, and to keep it useful rather than promotional.

Pinterest: an evergreen traffic engine

Pinterest keeps driving search traffic months after a pin is posted. The strategy is simple: create a pin for every blog post, then add more pins per post once you have a library of content. The traffic compounds slowly but reliably, and it is one of the most underused platforms for content-led brands.

Google Search Console: free indexing management

Canonical tags, sitemap submission, URL inspection and indexing requests. It is essential from day one on every site we launch. It is free, powerful and ignored by far too many people who build websites. If you are new to it, our website SEO FAQ is a good place to start.

Publishing and products

Amazon KDP: free self-publishing

Print on demand with no upfront cost and global distribution from day one. The royalty on a children’s paperback is modest, but the reach is immediate. For Mavericks Adventures, KDP removed the barrier between having a book and having a book on sale everywhere. See our KDP guide for the detail.

Gumroad and Payhip: digital product storefronts

Atker Consultancy sells its digital tools through Gumroad. We have also reviewed Payhip in depth, including its plans and fees, in our Payhip review for UK sellers. Both let you sell digital downloads without building a shop yourself. For a step-by-step process, read how to sell digital products online.

Ko-fi: a tip jar and support page

Almost no friction to set up, and useful as a low-key support option once an audience exists. It is not a primary income source for us, just an option for anyone who wants to support the work.

Analytics

Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console go on every site from day one. Both are free and both are essential: GA4 for traffic behaviour, Search Console for indexing and search visibility. We have not needed a paid analytics tool at this stage.
