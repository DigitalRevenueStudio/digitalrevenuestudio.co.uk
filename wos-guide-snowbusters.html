Snowbusters:
Fuel, Gear
and Clearing

How Snowbusters areas, fuel, health and coal work, what each gear piece does and where published guides disagree.

Overview

How Snowbusters Works

In Snowbusters you drive a plow through snow areas. Clearing an area to 100 percent unlocks the next one, and a finished area cannot be revisited. You can stop in the middle of an area and pick it up later. Guides describe a Fire in each area, where you deposit Coal to upgrade it. A stronger Fire clears a larger radius, raises the temperature and widens the Safe Zone where you recover health.

📅
Length

Two guides say the event runs for three days. The others do not give a length, so check your event screen.

🎁
Rewards

Guides list gems, theme items, map collections and fragments, skins and frames. Check your own reward screen for what is on offer.

⭐
Bonus areas

One guide says every fifth area (5, 10, 15 and 20) gives unlimited fuel while you are inside it.

🚜
Superplow

Two guides say it unlocks at Area 24, or when your state reaches Season 3. One guide measured manual clearing as cheaper in almost every area.

We found no entry requirement in the guides we read, and none of them describes how many runs you get each day. Check your event screen for both.

Gear

The Four Gear Pieces

You upgrade gear with Snowbuster Toolkits, which come from area rewards and Snowbuster Backpack cards. Gear resets at the end of each event, so only invest what you will use now.

PieceWhat a guide says it doesNotes
GlovesAdds health and reduces fuel consumptionHelps you clear for longer
CoatAdds damage reduction and faster fuel recoveryOne guide ties it to recovery speed. No guide says it controls your total fuel
SnowbusterAdds attack and clearing rangeHelps with enemies and wider sweeps
BootsAdds movement speed and healing near the FireUseful if you spend a lot of time travelling

The guides do not agree on an order. One lists Gloves, then Coat, then Snowbuster, then Boots. Another says there is no fixed order and suggests Snowbuster and Gloves early for hands-on play. A third says to upgrade when the green arrow shows. Choose by what limits you. If you keep running out of fuel, look at Gloves and Coat. If enemies hurt you, look at the damage and health pieces.

Clearing

How to Clear Efficiently

  • Rescue survivors early. All four guides we read say to do this. One says survivors join you and extend your clearing, for example by 10 percent.
  • Use a rocket near the end of an area. Three guides say to clear to about 95 percent and then use a rocket to finish. One guide values a V2 rocket at about 300 fuel and says to fire it only when it saves a long detour. Check what your rocket does before you rely on this.
  • Plan a route. One guide says to sweep in lanes from edge to edge. Another says to open a lane towards distant snow first and come back to the Fire later. Try both and see which clears more for your fuel.
  • Weigh Fire upgrades. Each upgrade costs coal that could turn into fuel at the end of the area. Upgrade when the larger radius or Safe Zone is worth more to you than the fuel.
  • Pick your fights. Plowing over snowmen can reveal fuel and coal, and attacking costs fuel. Beasts bite back and can drop theme map fragments. Fight when the reward or the route justifies it, since missed targets are lost once an area is finished.
Where Guides Differ

Where the Guides Disagree

  • Fuel numbers. One guide gives a recovery of 8 every 5 minutes and calls the older figure of 12 outdated. The others give no number.
  • Gear order. Three different approaches, as described above.
  • The final area. One guide calls Area 25 the maximum. Another says no maximum is published and a third covers Areas 1 to 23. Check your own event.
  • Route and rockets. Advice differs, and one guide measured manual clearing as cheaper than rockets in most areas.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Fighting everything. Every attack costs fuel and health. Fight what blocks you or pays for itself.

Backtracking. Plan a route so you are not crossing cleared snow.

Hitting zero health. You may lose the coal you carry, according to one guide.

Spending on gear without a reason. Gear resets after the event. Upgrade what limits your runs.

Treating numbers as fixed. Fuel figures and area limits differ between guides, so read your own screen.

Check on your own screens. The event length, your fuel meter and recovery rate, what each piece of gear says it does, how many runs you get, and what your rockets do. The details here come from published guides and some could not be confirmed.
