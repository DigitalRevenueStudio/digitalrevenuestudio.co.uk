Economy and rewards

Event Shops:
When to Spend
and When to Save

Each event has its own currency. This guide covers the main ones, what carries over, what shops sell and how to choose what to buy.

Earlier versions of this guide treated all event currency as one kind of token that expires when the event ends, said gems can be bought in event stores and ranked hero shards first for everyone. We found no support for those points. Each event or shop has its own currency, some of them carry over, and no guide we read lists gems for sale. This version says what we could confirm.

⚡ Quick Summary
What It Is
Each event or shop has its own currency, not one shared token
Core Rule
Check whether a currency carries over before you spend it
Best Habit
Pick a target and look through the whole shop first
Biggest Mistake
Spending on the first thing you see
Overview

How Event Shops Work

Many events and features have a shop where you spend a currency you earn by taking part. There is no single event token. The currencies below come from published guides and differ in where they are earned, whether they carry over and what they buy. The shop contents change between events and states, and the guides give few exact prices, so we describe the pattern and tell you to check your own store.

Other shops exist, such as General Trade, the Arena shop and the VIP shop, with their own coins and medals. One guide says the VIP shop takes Gems as payment, which is the reverse of buying them.

Stores usually set a daily or total limit on each item. The guides we read do not give the numbers, so look at the limit shown beside each item.

Spend or Save

Deciding When to Spend

  • Check carryover first. Silver Shells are reported to stay in your backpack, so waiting is safe for them. For other currencies the guides are silent, so look at the event information before you assume.
  • Look through the whole store. Two guides say to scroll the entire shop before you buy. Prices and items differ between events, and the best value is not always at the top.
  • Pick a target. Decide what you are saving for, for example a Fire Crystal bundle or a piece of hero gear, and buy toward that.
  • Do not save forever. One guide warns that holding currency indefinitely can delay permanent upgrades you could have now.
  • Check limits each day. Daily limits reset, so a limited item you skipped may be available again.
Priorities

What to Buy First

The guides disagree, so choose by what limits your account. One older guide says Fire Crystals or Design Plans are the best buys, then Mithril, and everything else is poor value unless you want cosmetics. Another ranks island buildings first, then rare hero gear, Chief materials, Fire Crystals, pet materials and finally speedups and Steel. That second ranking is for a specific event shop, and we cannot say it applies to others.

A practical way to decide is to ask what is slowing you down right now: a building that needs Fire Crystals, a hero you are building, or a materials shortage. Buy that first. Cosmetics have no effect on progress, so most players leave them until they have bought what they need. Resource packs are worth little if you can gather the resource, as our Resource Management guide explains.

Community Feedback

What Players Report

These are player opinions, not guaranteed best practice.

  • Buy for a goal. Players say a clear target stops you drifting into small purchases you do not need.
  • Keep a small reserve. One guide advises keeping some Alliance Tokens in reserve and checking upcoming events before you spend.
Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Assuming all currency expires. Some carry over. Check the one you hold.

Looking for gems in the store. No guide we read lists gems for sale.

Buying the first appealing item. Look through the whole store first.

Skipping the daily reset. Limits reset, so check again each day.

Copying another player's ranking. Shops and accounts differ. Buy what limits you.

Check on your own screens. Which currency you hold, whether it carries over, the limit and price beside each item, and what the store lists for your state. The details here come from published guides and some are single source.
