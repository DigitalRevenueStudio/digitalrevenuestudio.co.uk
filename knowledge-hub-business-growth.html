About this subject

What you will find here

This is where most of our own experience sits. We have sold digital products on Payhip and Gumroad, taken books through Amazon KDP in paperback, hardback and Kindle, and built brands where the first product is only the first step. The guides here cover what to sell, where to sell it, what the platforms really charge and what happens after the first sale.

One lesson runs through all of it: a good product with no visitors sells nothing. Our first products had 48 views across two platforms and no sales in the early months. The product was not the problem, traffic was. So these guides talk about finding buyers as much as making the thing you sell.

We are a UK studio, so tax, VAT, refunds and pricing are written from a UK point of view. None of it is financial or tax advice. Where a decision depends on your own circumstances, we say so and point you to the official source.

Common questions

Quick answers

The questions we are asked most on this subject, answered briefly, with a link to the full guide.

Is Payhip legit?

Yes. It is a long-running UK platform that independent creators use to sell digital products. Payments go through Stripe or PayPal, and every seller is independent and sets their own refund policy.

Read the Payhip review →

Do I have to offer refunds on digital products?

In the UK the 14-day right to cancel can end once a download starts, but only if the buyer agreed to immediate access first. Faulty files must still be put right, so set out your policy before people buy.

Read the digital products FAQ →

Why are children’s book royalties lower on KDP?

Full-colour interiors cost more to print, and the printing cost comes straight off your share of each paperback sale. The more colour pages, the lower your royalty at any given price.
