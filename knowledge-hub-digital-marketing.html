About this subject

What you will find here

A lot of marketing advice assumes a team and a budget. Most of the brands we work with have neither, so this subject sticks to what one small business can realistically run: a website Google can understand, an email list you own, and a social presence that sends people somewhere useful.

We run email for several brands on a free plan, set up Google Search Console on every site we build and check it every week. We have learned the hard way which pages Google ignores, why a page can be crawled and still not indexed, and how long it really takes for a new site to be trusted. The articles here share that, including the parts that did not work.

If you are starting from nothing, begin with email. A list you own keeps working when a platform changes its rules, and our Sender review explains how we run ours without paying for it. Then make sure your site is set up so Google can find it, which our website and SEO FAQ covers in plain English.

Common questions

Quick answers

The questions we are asked most on this subject, answered briefly, with a link to the full guide.

Is Sender really free?

The free plan covers automation, tagging, the form builder and the email editor. What you give up is mainly advanced analytics, some templates and a small Sender badge in the footer of your emails.

Read the Sender review →

How long does SEO take?

Months, not days. A new site has to be discovered, crawled and trusted. Useful content that is kept up to date compounds over time, so plan it as part of the build rather than an afterthought.

Read the SEO FAQ →

Do I need a sitemap?

Yes. A sitemap lists the pages you want search engines to find. It helps Google discover new pages and understand how your site fits together, and you submit it once in Search Console.
