From Dining Room Idea to Published Book

A client came to us with a concept they'd been sitting on for a year, a children's book starring their cockapoo. Here is how we took it from a dining room conversation to a published brand on Amazon KDP.

6 min read Digital Revenue Studio March 2026

A client approached Digital Revenue Studio with an idea they'd been discussing at home for the better part of a year. They wanted to write a children's book built around their cockapoo, a character with a real personality and a natural gift for getting into adventures. The concept was there. The execution had never happened.

Our brief was to take it from that dining room conversation to a fully realised brand on Amazon, logo, website, publishing, content and all the infrastructure in between. Here is how we did it.

The brief we set ourselves

Most KDP publishing advice focuses on the platform mechanics, how to upload, how to price, how to get reviews. We took a different approach with this client: build the character first and treat the book as the entry point to something bigger.

The book was never going to be a standalone product. It was the first asset in a character-led brand, one where the IP could grow into a series, activity packs, social content and more over time.

“Treat the character as the primary asset. The book is just the first thing you can buy.”

Building the brand before the book

Before a single page was designed, we built the brand infrastructure for the client. Logo, colour palette, website, social identity. The brand needed to feel warm, adventurous and consistent whether a reader found it on Amazon, Instagram or the website.

The website launched on GitHub Pages at mavericksadventures.co.uk, connected via IONOS DNS. Free hosting, custom domain, indexed on Google from day one. GA4 and Search Console active before launch.

A separate Sender email account was set up for the brand, distinct from other studio projects. Parents who sign up want character content, not studio build updates.

Publishing on Amazon KDP

The client's book was published on Amazon KDP UK in both paperback and Kindle format. No upfront printing costs. Available across UK, US and international Amazon marketplaces from day one. That global reach with zero print investment is the core KDP proposition for any new brand.

The results: The book launched across UK, US and international Amazon marketplaces. Early sales, reviews and royalty data are documented in full on the Mavericks Adventures case study.

KDP royalties are modest per copy but compound across a series. The model works when you treat it as a long-term brand build rather than a single title. That is exactly the brief we were given and the approach we took.

Content and traffic

Seven blog posts went live in the first two months, each SEO-targeted around terms parents and gift-buyers actually search. Pinterest is active and driving evergreen traffic, pins continue to generate visits months after publishing.

An activity pack was built and hosted directly on GitHub, free download, no email required. It serves as a free entry point to the brand and a tool that converts curious visitors into book buyers.

What we learned

Building the brand infrastructure before the book launched was the right call. By the time the book was available on Amazon, there was already a website to send readers to, a social identity to build recognition around and an email list beginning to grow.

The character model works because it is not limited to one book. Every future title adds to the same brand. That compounding is slow at first but it builds in a way a standalone title cannot, and that is the kind of long-term thinking we bring to every brand brief we take on.

The full numbers and timeline are documented in the case study. This is a 12-24 month brand build, not a 90-day result. The foundations are in place.
