The Basics

What Does NAP Mean?

NAP stands for Non-Aggression Pact. In Whiteout Survival it is an agreement between the alliances in a state that they will not attack each other, or will only attack each other under agreed conditions.

The important part is that players make it, not the game. Nothing in the game stops an attack on a NAP member. The pact works because alliance leaders agree the rules, tell their members and deal with anyone who breaks them.

Terms

NAP10, NAP5 and State-Wide NAP

TermWhat it usually meansWorth knowing
NAP10The top ten alliances agree not to attack each otherThe most common version. The list is usually based on alliance ranking
NAP5The top five alliances onlySmaller alliances outside the list are not covered
State-wide NAPEveryone in the state is coveredUsually comes with stricter rules for events and farms
NAP listThe state's agreed list of protected alliancesCan include academy or farm alliances. Always check the current list

The word "usually" matters. The number tells you roughly who is covered, but not the full rules. Two states can both run NAP10 and still have very different rules.

The Rules

What a NAP Usually Covers

Every state writes its own rules. These are the areas they normally cover, with examples of what states agree.

AreaExamples of what states agree
City attacksNo attacks on protected players' cities
Gathering troopsNo hitting troops on resource tiles, or only during certain events
ScoutingAllowed, discouraged or banned
Alliance territoryNo attacks on banners or alliance headquarters
Farm and academy alliancesNamed as protected, or left out
Kill eventsKept in place, limited (for example tiles only) or paused for the event
Facilities and fortressesShared out on a rota, or fought over under agreed rules
Breaking the rulesHow to report it and what the penalty is

Find your state's rules. Ask your R4 or R5 where the NAP rules are posted. Many states pin them in state chat or an alliance announcement.

Kill Events

Does a Kill Event Cancel NAP?

Not automatically. A kill event only changes the NAP if your state has agreed that it does. Some states allow fighting on resource tiles only, some allow a limited number of city attacks, and some keep full protection throughout.

So be careful with rules of thumb such as "anything goes in a kill event" or "gatherers are always fair game". They may be true in one state and break the pact in another.

Why States Use It

Why Run a NAP at All?

🤝
The case for it

Fighting inside your own state burns troops and resources that could be used against other states in SvS. A NAP lets alliances grow, cooperate on events and arrive at SvS stronger.

⚖️
The trade-off

A NAP can also lock in the top alliances' position and leave smaller alliances outside the list with less protection and fewer opportunities. Players who enjoy fighting can find a strict NAP frustrating.

Neither view is wrong. It is state politics, and it is worth knowing which kind of state you are joining, especially before a state transfer.

If It Happens

What to Do If Someone Breaks NAP

1
Save the evidence. Keep the battle report and take screenshots.
2
Note who was involved. Record the player name and alliance tag.
3
Check the rules. Make sure it was not allowed, for example during an agreed event window.
4
Report it to your leaders. Send the evidence to your R4 or R5.
5
Do not hit back on your own. Let the leaders sort it out. Retaliating yourself can turn one incident into a war between alliances.
Across States

How NAP and SvS Connect

NAP keeps the peace inside your state. SvS is the competition against another state. They work together: a state that is not fighting itself usually goes into SvS with more troops and resources.

But your NAP only covers players in your own state. During SvS, players from the opposing state are not part of it and can attack you if they can reach you. If you will be offline during the battle, use a shield. Our SvS guide covers how the event runs.

Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Treating NAP as a shield. It is a player agreement. It does not stop the attack, so shield when you need protection.

Assuming every state has the same rules. NAP10 in one state can work very differently from NAP10 in another.

Expecting it to cover SvS. Players from the opposing state are not part of your NAP.

Hitting back straight away. Report it with evidence and let your leaders handle it.

Not checking the current list. Alliances move in and out of the top ten. Check who is covered before you attack anyone.

FAQ

NAP Questions

What does NAP mean in Whiteout Survival?

NAP stands for Non-Aggression Pact. It is an agreement between players and alliances in a state not to attack each other, set and enforced by the state's leaders rather than by the game.

What is NAP10?

NAP10 usually means the top ten alliances in the state have agreed not to attack each other. NAP5 would usually be the top five. Each state decides exactly who is covered, so ask your R4s for your state's list.

Does NAP protect me during SvS?

No. Your state's NAP is an agreement between players in your own state. Players from the opposing state are not part of it, so use a shield if you will be offline during the cross-state battle.

Is breaking NAP against the game rules?

No. The game allows the attack. Breaking NAP breaks an agreement between players, so it is handled by the state's leaders, not by the game or support.

Every state is different. This guide explains how NAPs usually work. Your state's own rules, as set by its leaders, are the ones that count.
