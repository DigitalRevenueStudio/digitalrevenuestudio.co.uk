Pure Pet Food Review: What Happened When Our Cockapoo Switched to Fresh Food

A UK owner’s account of switching a cockapoo from dry kibble to Pure Pet Food: ordering, ingredients, how the dog responded and the one honest downside.

This review was written with the Mavericks Adventures family, whose cockapoo, Maverick, is the star of their children’s books. Digital Revenue Studio helped structure the post and set up the affiliate link. The experience is theirs, and the words below are in their voice.

At a glance: Pure Pet Food is a UK fresh dog food service with a personalised plan and pre-portioned daily pouches. Our cockapoo moved over from dry kibble in about a week. The downside is cost, which is higher than kibble.

We have a cockapoo called Maverick. He is energetic, curious and had been on standard dry kibble since we got him. My wife had been reading about fresh dog food for a while and, after some research, we decided to try Pure Pet Food. This is what happened.

“My wife’s verdict after the first delivery: ‘I wish someone had told us about this sooner.’”

Ordering and delivery

Pure Pet Food asks a series of questions when you sign up: your dog’s breed, age, weight, activity level and any sensitivities. From those answers it calculates a recommended daily portion and builds a personalised plan. For a cockapoo like Maverick the process took about five minutes.

The first delivery arrived faster than expected and was packaged properly for fresh food: insulated, with everything clearly labelled. My wife reacted to the packaging before she had opened anything. It felt considered rather than generic, the kind of packaging that tells you the company has thought carefully about what it is doing.

The food comes in pre-portioned daily pouches. No measuring, no guesswork, no scooping. You open the pouch and serve it. For anyone who has spent time weighing kibble to the gram, that is a noticeable simplification.

Ingredients and what impressed us

The ingredient list is short. Real meat, in Maverick’s case chicken, comes first, followed by vegetables and a small number of added vitamins and minerals. No obvious fillers and nothing that needs a chemistry degree to interpret.

My wife spent longer reading the ingredient panel than I expected. Her background is in nutrition for people rather than dogs, but the principles transfer. She looked at the balance of protein to carbohydrate, whether the meat source was named specifically rather than listed generically, and whether the overall formulation made sense for an active dog.

Her conclusion was direct: this is noticeably better than what we had been feeding him. Not marginally. Noticeably.

How Maverick responded

We introduced the new food gradually over about a week, mixing it with his existing kibble and increasing the share of Pure Pet Food each day. Dogs can have sensitive stomachs when their food changes, so we did not rush it.

By day three the bowl was being cleaned faster than it ever had been on kibble. By the end of the first week the switch was complete. There is an enthusiasm at mealtimes now that was not there before, which is either the food itself or the fact that it smells much more like real food than a bag of dry biscuits.

After three weeks we noticed his coat looked better: shinier and softer. Whether that is down to the food or coincidence we cannot say with scientific certainty, but the timing is notable. If you are thinking of changing your dog’s diet, it is sensible to talk to your vet first, particularly if your dog has health issues or a sensitive stomach.

The honest verdict

What worked

Personalised plan, pre-portioned pouches, a short and clean ingredient list, fast delivery and impressive packaging.

What stood out

My wife’s reaction. She is not easily impressed by marketing, and the product won her over before the first bag was finished.

Maverick’s verdict

Bowl cleaned every time, more enthusiasm at mealtimes and a better-looking coat after three weeks.

The honest negative

It costs more than kibble. That is the only real downside, and whether it is worth it depends on what matters to you as a dog owner.

Pure Pet Food questions

Is Pure Pet Food good for cockapoos?

It suited ours. The plan is built around breed, age, weight and activity, so portions are matched to the individual dog. Every dog is different, so check with your vet if yours has particular health needs.

How long does it take to switch from kibble?

We took about a week, blending the new food in gradually. A slow change is kinder on the stomach than switching overnight.

Is Pure Pet Food more expensive than kibble?

Yes. Fresh food costs more than dry kibble, and prices and offers change, so check the current plan price for your dog’s size on the Pure Pet Food site before you commit.
