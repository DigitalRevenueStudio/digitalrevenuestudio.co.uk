Event Guide

Wild Brawl:
F2P Strategy,
Scoring and Airdrops

Wild Brawl is a free-for-all battlefield event where you score through objectives, not only by winning fights. This guide puts free-to-play players first: how to complete the missions, how supplies and airdrops work, which queue to try, how to approach the buildings and what stronger accounts can do differently.

Biggest Mistake
Treating it as a kill race

Wild Brawl rewards participation and progress, not just winning. The missions ask you to take part in several matches, use airdrop items and build up a seasonal score, so a sensible F2P plan is to collect supplies, use airdrops and take buildings you can realistically hold, rather than repeatedly attacking the strongest Chief in the lobby.

Event Overview

Wild Brawl at a Glance

⚔
Format

An individual free-for-all, normally about 10 Chiefs per battlefield. You use your own troops and march queues, and the aim is to earn the most Strategic Points.

🔓
Requirements

Furnace Level 25 or higher, plus your state needs to be old enough, so check the in-game event page for the exact unlock. It is not available before Hero Generation 3, and we do not give a universal unlock day, because Furnace 25 alone does not guarantee access.

🎟
Attempts

10 matches across the event, not 10 per day. A match only uses an attempt once matchmaking succeeds and you enter the battlefield.

⏰
Matchmaking Windows

There are four two-hour matchmaking periods each day over a two-day event. Check your live event page for your own times.

⏱
Match Length

About 10 minutes: roughly 30 seconds of preparation, then around nine and a half minutes of battle .

🏅
Shop Currency

Pioneer Badges. They are not recycled when the event ends, so you can save them.

Missions

Your First Priority: Complete the Missions

There are 10 missions across the two-day event. Check your live mission list before planning around these numbers, because they can change.

Mission groupTargetNotes
ParticipationTake part in 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 matchesYou need more than zero Strategic Points in each qualifying match, so entering and doing nothing may not count
Airdrop itemsUse 5, 10 and 15 airdropped itemsThe wording is “use”, so check your counter and actually activate items
Seasonal scoreAbout 25,000 Strategic Points across the seasonSpread across all your matches, not one match

Six reward matches does not mean six attempts

You have 10 attempts but the participation missions stop at six qualifying matches. The extra attempts give you room to finish the score and item missions, practise, or play competitively. The mission list does not show a separate reward for using all 10.

A simple planning benchmark

Six matches at around 4,000 to 4,500 points each would reach the seasonal target, and 15 item uses across six matches works out at roughly two or three per match. These are planning averages and progress checks, not quotas you must force. Some older community posts mention a different seasonal threshold, so trust your live mission screen.

Does finishing first give better rewards?

Rewards appear to come from the milestones, not from finishing rank, and community feedback says the leaderboard offers little for it. We have not seen the full reward structure confirmed, so open your reward tabs. If you cannot see a rank reward you value, do not spend scarce buffs chasing a high finish.

Our recommended F2P objective order

1
Earn some points in every matchAim for at least some Strategic Points in each match you want to count towards participation.
2
Collect and use airdrop itemsDo this throughout the event, and check the item counter after activating.
3
Build towards the seasonal scoreSupplies, airdrops and selective building captures all add up over the season.
4
Complete six qualifying matchesThis unlocks the participation missions.
5
Check what remainsDecide whether to use your other attempts for missions, practice or competition.

This order follows the mission list. It is our recommendation, not a game rule.

Before You Enter

Preparation Checklist

🚫
Clear Entry Restrictions

You cannot enter while troops are scouting or on an expedition that blocks entry, while your barricade is burning, or while another Chief has reinforcements in your Embassy. Sort these before the window you plan to use.

🕒
Leave Time To Finish

Allow for matchmaking plus a 10-minute battle. A successful match uses an attempt, and fully quitting can have consequences beyond returning to your city (see the warning below).

🧭
Prepare For Objectives

Have one reliable combat march, know which other marches can collect supplies, and avoid spreading your best heroes so thinly that no march can contest anything.

🧪
Be Careful With Buffs

10% and 20% combat buffs and battle-related pet bonuses work here, while some bonuses are disabled. We do not recommend spending scarce buffs just to finish participation missions. Use them for a specific aim.

Matchmaking

Same-Gen or Cross-Gen?

The basic difference is simple. Same-Gen Battle matches you within your Hero Generation, while Cross-Gen Battle can match you with Chiefs from all generations.

QuestionSame-GenCross-Gen
Hero generation restrictionChiefs in the same Hero GenerationChiefs across generations
Equal spending or strength?Not promisedNot promised
Protection from later generations?Yes, through the generation restrictionNo, all generations are possible
Guaranteed easier opponents?NoNo

Same generation does not mean equal strength. The event uses your own troops and combat bonuses still apply, so do not treat Same-Gen as a standardised or spending-neutral battlefield.

Our recommended starting point

Established later-generation accountTest bothCross-Gen can include earlier accounts, but also strong later ones

Missions already completeEitherUse spare attempts to experiment

These are editorial recommendations based on the confirmed queue rules. Community feedback is mixed: some players say Cross-Gen suits older-state accounts and that heavy spenders prefer Same-Gen, while others recommend Same-Gen for a reasonably strong F2P account and warn lower-generation players away from Cross-Gen. Neither claim is backed by matchmaking data, and one unusually good or bad match proves little.

Community feedback: A few players suggest later windows or Day 2 are easier because competitive players may already have used their attempts. We have not seen anything to back this up. Try it if it suits you, but do not leave all your attempts to the final window.

Cancelling versus not readying up

Cancelling matchmaking has no penalty, but repeatedly failing to ready up after a match is found can temporarily block matchmaking. Cancelling a search is not the same as declining a match that has been found.

Battlefield and Scoring

Understanding the Points

The four buildings

The battlefield has one Twilight Citadel in the centre and three Boulder Fortresses around it. A Chief needs to occupy a building for about 10 seconds to gain control. Buildings give initial-capture points and ongoing control points, and the Citadel is worth more than a fortress. The centre is valuable, but that is exactly why it attracts the most competition.

The three scoring routes

RouteWhat it involvesOur recommended F2P role
Building controlCapture a building and keep itOpportunistic or sustained scoring when your march is competitive
Strategic suppliesCollect crates unloaded by the supply trucksYour main fallback when buildings are too contested
AirdropsCollect item crates and use battlefield itemsSupports the item missions and gives you tools for a realistic opening

Your Battle Record shows these three categories. Some short descriptions of the scoring only mention supply chests and airdrop crates, but buildings are scoring objectives too, so check your live building descriptions and Battle Record to confirm the current version.

Building values (community figures)

BuildingFirst-capture pointsOngoing points
Twilight CitadelAround 7,000Around 2,000 to 2,100 a minute
Each Boulder FortressAround 2,000Around 600 a minute

Community feedback. These figures come from the community, with a player report backing up the centre value. One source also says every Chief can earn their own first-capture bonus on each building. We have not verified that, so test it before you plan a route around visiting all four buildings, and do not treat a total of around 13,000 points as guaranteed.

Do kills score points?

Scoring focuses on buildings, supplies and airdrops, and we have not found a separate kill-point category. Treat fighting as a way to secure objectives, defend access or disrupt opponents, not as a score source on its own.

Supply Trucks

Your Lower-Conflict Scoring Route

Three supply trucks start entering about 30 seconds into the battle phase and then repeat every minute. They travel to three circular areas and unload strategic supply crates, which your troops collect for Strategic Points. Community feedback also points to gathering as a practical way to progress without being one of the strongest accounts.

Our routine

Find the three drop-off areas early, watch which gets the least attention, keep a march free for new supplies and move when a dominant player turns up.

Timing

Trucks take time to travel before unloading, so plan around what you can see on the battlefield, not the moment a truck enters.

Pets

Pet gathering skills do not apply in this event, so do not spend pet resources expecting a gathering boost.

Airdrops

Collect Consistently, Use Deliberately

Airdrops begin about 90 seconds after the battle begins and then drop roughly every two minutes. Each crate holds one random item, you can send only one squad to collect crates at a time, and there is no overall limit on how many items you can obtain. Follow the live timer on the battlefield, because the preparation countdown and the battle phase are easy to confuse.

Collecting is not necessarily using. Collecting a crate and using the item are described differently: collecting is named as a scoring source, using items is linked to airdrop points, and the mission says “use”. We are not certain exactly what counts in the current version, so check three things in your own event: whether collecting raised your score, whether using the item raised your score, and whether the mission counter moved. Until you have checked, activate your items instead of assuming a pickup is enough.

The four items

ItemEffect (community figures)Practical F2P use
Destroyer RocketSeverely injures a large number of troops (community figures suggest about 30,000) in an enemy city or garrisoned buildingWeaken a target just before an attack you could plausibly win
Frost BombStops a targeted city deploying troops for about 10 secondsDelay a response while you contest an objective
Smoke BombMakes your city invisible for up to about two minutes, and deploying troops removes itProtect your city during a genuine pause
Battlefield MedKitHeals about 30% of your injured troops, with a capRestore enough strength to keep scoring

The detailed numbers come mainly from community feedback, so check your live tooltips for the final figures and targeting rules.

Rockets are disruption, not an automatic win. Injuring a set number of troops does not remove a defender’s remaining stat advantage. Community players use rockets to wear down a strong player holding several objectives, and some suggest firing a second or two before your attack lands, but results depend on the garrison, your march and the opponent’s response.

Frost Bomb does not stop teleporting. A Chief hit by a Frost Bomb can still teleport. It also should not be assumed to cancel marches that were already sent.

Smoke Bomb ends when you deploy. Activating it just before you send another march wastes most of its protection. Teleporting remains possible under it, and it is not shown to protect separate garrisons.

MedKit timing is a trade-off. Some players say wait until injuries are very high to get the most from the percentage heal, others say heal before your army stops being useful. Our recommendation is to heal when the recovered troops will let you keep collecting, defend something worthwhile or make a realistic capture.

Community feedback: Rocket stacking can create a capture opportunity, especially against an overextended player, but it is not a guaranteed way to beat a stronger account. Test it on realistic targets and compare it with simply carrying on gathering. Do not hold every item until the final seconds waiting for a perfect combination, particularly while the item missions are unfinished.

Troops, healing and permanent losses

The battlefield has unlimited Infirmary capacity, healing costs no resources, MedKits are the in-battle healing method, troops heal automatically when you leave, and you cannot loot resources from defeated Chiefs. That makes it quite different from ordinary map PvP, so you do not need to treat every fight as a permanent loss. It also means normal healing resources and speedups are not needed here. “Free” healing still does not mean instant healing during the match, so MedKit supply affects how long your troops stay useful.

City attacks, burning and relocation

Cities burn faster on the battlefield, and if a defence fails and the fire continues you can be forcibly relocated. A defeat is not elimination, but relocation can interrupt your routes. If a stronger player keeps hitting your city, stop contesting their strongest position, check your troops, consider a Smoke Bomb if it fits your next action, relocate when the event allows it and go back to scoring somewhere quieter. Some players report free teleporting with a short cooldown, but we have not confirmed that, so check the in-game teleport button.

Heroes and Formations

No Verified Best Lineup

We have not seen testing that establishes one best Wild Brawl lineup for every generation, so we do not name one. Please do not copy a generic PvP, Bear Hunt or spender lineup and treat it as proven for this event.

A testing framework

Start with your strongest developed expedition-combat lineup, check which skills apply here, compare real battle reports against similar opponents, keep one strong march for objectives you can contest, and avoid breaking a reliable lineup into several weak marches.

Read the wording

Some players warn that city-defence widget effects should not automatically be assumed to apply to garrisoned buildings. Read the actual wording before you rely on a bonus.

Troop ratios

We have not seen Wild Brawl-specific ratio testing, so we do not give a ratio. An attack march, a garrison and a supply march have different jobs, so test against comparable opposition.

Match Plan

A Practical Plan for F2P Players

This is an editorial playbook built from the objectives and community strategies. It is a starting point, not a script that guarantees a win.

Preparation phase

Use the first 30 seconds to look at the battlefield, find the fortresses and supply areas, decide whether you can fight for buildings and pick a fallback route. Do not judge your strategy by displayed power alone, because total power can be inflated by troop numbers.

Opening

If you look competitive, try an accessible building. If you are clearly outmatched, position for supplies and airdrops instead of feeding your main march into the centre.

Middle

Keep asking whether the building is still worth contesting, whether supplies or an airdrop are available, whether an item improves a realistic opportunity, and whether you need to heal first. Do not spend the whole match trying to reverse one losing fight.

Final phase

Use items while they can still matter, finish collections, and avoid long chases with little chance of points. Do not abandon an easy objective for one last attack on the leader.

After the match

Open your Battle Record and note your total points, how much came from buildings, gathering and airdrops, your mission counters, whether fighting helped or interrupted scoring, and one change to try next match.

Building Control

Tactics for F2P Players

Winning the opening fight is not enough on its own, because your garrison has to survive the control period. After each capture ask whether you can survive the next likely attack, whether the points justify defending, and whether you need the building or mainly need more mission progress. For an F2P player, leaving a losing position can be the right decision.

Side-fortress anchor

For a competitive march that cannot hold the centre. Take an accessible side fortress, complete the control timer, gather with spare capacity while it is quiet and keep a response ready. Leave if repeated losses stop paying off. The aim is steady scoring, not proving you can hold one building forever.

Capture and withdraw

For when you cannot sustain a long defence. Watch for an empty or weakened building, check you can survive the timer, use items if they really help, then reassess before defending it.

Hit the weaker secondary march

A player holding several buildings may have a weaker march at some of them. Look at the less contested positions, test a realistic target and stop if the gap is bigger than expected. Player reports say this can work, but not that every multi-building holder is vulnerable.

Contest the centre selectively

Worth it when you are competitive with the garrison, the building is empty, the holder is weakened or items create a real opening. Less sensible if repeated attacks give no control while supplies go uncollected.

City pressure as disruption

An advanced, situational tactic. Players describe pressuring a strong opponent’s city while contesting objectives, though others report it still failed against a strong defender. Do not assume relocation ejects every garrison.

Stronger Accounts and Spenders

Convert Strength Into Objective Control

This section is for players with stronger combat development, deeper troop reserves or several developed marches. Spending does not guarantee dominance in your lobby, so use real battle results rather than the label to decide what fits. It is a separate approach from the F2P plan above, and not a reason to change it.

Citadel first

For a player clearly competitive with the strongest opposing march. Contest the centre early with your best lineup, finish the timer, then watch for incoming attacks instead of assuming the opening win settles control.

Expand only as far as you can defend

Test one secondary fortress, check how your second march performs, and withdraw from weak positions before opponents keep exploiting them. Controlling everything can invite rocket pressure.

Keep your best march for the hard objective

If a building is quiet, a less valuable march may hold it while your main formation takes the contested one. This is community advice, so follow the garrison interface carefully and avoid a control gap.

Buffs for a defined goal

Confirm a bonus actually applies, decide which contest it is for, and check your reward screen before spending to chase rank. President-related and territory bonuses are disabled.

Defend against attrition

Even a strong account can lose effectiveness to repeated injuries and divided attention. Track important garrisons, collect MedKits and disruption items, and avoid chasing every attacker.

Once missions are done

Play for a personal best, try a different queue or simply enjoy a competitive fight. These are playstyle choices, not reward requirements.

How to test disputed advice

Change one thing at a time. Compare the two queues while keeping your playstyle similar, a gathering-led match against a building-led match, your airdrop counter before collecting, after collecting and after using, whether recapturing a building you held gives another bonus, and your troop recovery straight after a MedKit. A few matches can show what works for your account, but they cannot prove a universal rule.

Leaving the Battlefield

An Important Warning

Read the exit prompt carefully. Temporarily returning to your city and fully quitting Wild Brawl are different actions. Fully quitting can mean you can only take part in the next event season, although some community feedback describes a shorter restriction. Do not fully quit just to escape a difficult lobby, and do not assume you can queue again straight away.

Pioneer Badge Shop

F2P Spending Guidance

Pioneer Badges are not recycled when the event ends, so you do not have to force a purchase. Shop availability depends partly on state progression, and some items are restricted to the Fire Crystal Age. Community feedback mentions refinement materials, Fire Crystals, charm materials and hero gear chests, so check your own inventory and prices.

Some players recommend Refined Fire Crystals first because they are scarce. That is purchasing advice, not a verified best value for every account, and an account that cannot use them soon may reasonably choose something else. Common Mistakes

What Most Players Get Wrong

Treating it as a kill race. The scoring is objective-based, so fights only help when they give you access to objectives.

Assuming Same-Gen equalises strength. It restricts hero generation, not power or spending.

Hitting a player you cannot dislodge. Repeatedly attacking while supplies sit uncollected elsewhere is one of the most common ways to waste a match.

Collecting items but not checking the use counter. Make sure the “use” mission has actually moved.

Breaking your own Smoke Bomb. Deploying straight after activating it ends the protection.

Assuming Frost Bomb stops teleporting. It does not.

Confusing six milestones with six attempts. You have 10 attempts, and only six matches are needed for the participation missions.

Rushing to spend Pioneer Badges. They are not recycled when the event ends.

Fully quitting without reading the warning. It can limit you to the next event season.

Quick Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a F2P player get useful rewards without winning? The missions rely on participation, item use and cumulative points, not first place. Community feedback also reports completing objectives through gathering and airdrops.

Do I need 25,000 points in one match? No. It is a seasonal target across the event. Check your live mission wording in case it changes.

Can I enter and do nothing for participation rewards? The participation missions need more than zero Strategic Points, so merely entering may not count.

Can I get more than one airdrop item per match? Yes. There is no overall limit, although you can send only one collection squad at a time.

Can I heal with normal resources? Healing during the match is MedKit-only, costs no resources and happens automatically when you leave.

Can the winner loot my resources? No. Winning an attack against another Chief does not let you loot resources on the battlefield.

Are President and territory bonuses active? No, they are disabled. Combat buffs and battle-related pet bonuses do work.

Should I use every attempt? Use enough to complete the missions you value. Anything left over is for practice or enjoyment.

Quick Checklist

Before, During and After

Before matchmaking

Check the live rules, mission targets and queue windows. Clear scouting, expedition, burning-city and Embassy restrictions. Pick a queue. Leave enough time. Keep scarce buffs for a clear reason.

During the match

Earn some points. Adapt to your opponents instead of forcing a centre rush. Watch the supply routes. Collect and use airdrop items. Heal while recovered troops can still help. Read any exit prompt.

After the match

Check your Battle Record and mission counters. Note what produced your points. Change one thing for next time. Claim finished rewards. Save Pioneer Badges if the shop has nothing useful.
