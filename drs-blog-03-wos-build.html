How a Viral Reddit Post Accidentally Became a Gaming Wiki

The Whiteout Survival wiki was never part of the plan. It exists because a Reddit post travelled further than we expected and a player’s comment gave us the brief.

6 min readDigital Revenue StudioMarch 2026

The Whiteout Survival wiki was never meant to be a product. Digital Revenue Studio set out to help brands build useful websites and content, not to run a gaming reference site. The wiki exists because of a Reddit post that travelled much further than anyone expected, and a single comment that told us what to build next.

The post that showed us what Reddit could do

Early on, one of our most useful posts, a plain-English guide to challenging a council tax band, was shared in r/UKPersonalFinance and went viral. It had no promotion behind it and no existing audience. It was simply a useful answer posted in the right community at the right time.

The reach was the lesson. Reddit rewards genuine contribution, and a post that solves a real problem can travel a very long way. We started looking at where else a useful guide might do the same.

A beginner’s guide for a game we play

One of those places was Whiteout Survival, a mobile strategy game we play ourselves. We had been working on a paid guide, and in January 2026 we posted a free beginner’s guide to r/whiteoutsurvival to see whether anyone wanted it. Within 48 hours it had thousands of views and a lively comment thread.

The comments were the real prize, because they told us exactly what the community needed. One comment in particular changed the direction of the project:

“There are hundreds of guides like this online, just not put into a single wiki.”, ChandlerOG, r/whiteoutsurvival

That comment became the brief. We had planned to sell a guide. The community told us they would rather have everything in one free place, so we built that instead. The full story of that decision is in We Started Building a Paid WOS Guide. Reddit Changed That.

Building the wiki

The wiki lives on the Digital Revenue Studio website rather than on a separate platform or third-party wiki tool. It is clean HTML pages organised by category, indexed by Google from the start. It launched with a first set of guides on the most requested topics, including Bear Hunt, Frostfire Mine, SvS preparation, event tokens and gift codes, and it has grown steadily since.

Every guide is free, with no login, no paywall and no email address required to read. Free-first is the whole strategy: build trust with the community by giving it something genuinely useful, with no strings attached. Everything else follows from that trust.

Community before content

The WOS approach is community first. We do not publish content and hope players find it. We take part in the community and let the content come from what players actually ask for.

The most significant development came after we reached out directly to ChandlerOG, the player whose comment had shaped the wiki. He shared it with his alliance, WAO in State 1389, one of the strongest alliances across neighbouring states. Feedback from players at that level is invaluable for a guide that aims to be useful at every level of play.

What the wiki taught us

Three lessons stand out. Find a community that already exists. Give it something genuinely useful. And do not ask for anything in return until there is enough trust to make asking appropriate. The wiki grew because we listened to what players asked for rather than publishing what we assumed they needed, and it only exists because we were paying attention when a post went further than planned.
