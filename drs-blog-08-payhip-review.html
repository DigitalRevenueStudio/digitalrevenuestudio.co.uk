Payhip Review UK 2026: Is It Legit? Fees, Pros & Cons

A plain-English review of Payhip for UK sellers: what it costs, whether it is safe to use, where it falls short and when another platform makes more sense. We use it ourselves, and this is what we found.

6 min read Digital Revenue Studio March 2026 Updated October 2026

If you want to sell ebooks, templates, guides or other digital products, Payhip is one of the first names you will come across. It is also one of the most searched questions we see: is Payhip legit, what does it cost and is it any good for someone in the UK? This review answers all three, based on using it to sell our own products.

The short version: Payhip is a real, long-running platform with a free plan, clean checkout and automatic file delivery. It is not a marketplace, so it will not find you customers. Whether it is the right choice depends on how much you plan to sell and where your traffic comes from. If you want to try it, you can start on the free plan here (affiliate link, see the disclosure below).

What is Payhip?

Payhip is an e-commerce platform built for digital products: ebooks, guides, templates, courses, memberships and software. You create a storefront, upload your files and Payhip handles the checkout, payment processing and file delivery. The customer pays, the file is sent automatically and you do not need to touch anything.

It is designed to be the simplest route from “I have something to sell” to “someone can buy it”, and for the most part it delivers on that.

Is Payhip Legit and Safe?

Yes. Payhip is an established, UK-based platform used by a large number of independent creators, and it processes payments through Stripe and PayPal, so card details are handled by those payment providers rather than by the seller. As a buyer, you are paying through a normal checkout. As a seller, you are paid out to your own Stripe or PayPal account.

A few honest caveats apply to any platform of this kind. Payhip is not a marketplace, so nobody is vetting your products or sending you shoppers. You are responsible for describing your products accurately and for customer service. On tax, Payhip says it automatically collects, reports and remits EU and UK VAT on digital products, as well as US and Canadian sales tax, on your behalf, which takes a real burden off a small seller. Tax on your own income is still yours to handle, so take advice for your situation.

Payhip also runs an affiliate programme for referring new sellers. We are part of it, which is why this page contains affiliate links, and we say so clearly below. We were using Payhip before we applied, and our view of the platform would be the same either way.

When does upgrading make sense?

Because the processing fees are the same on every plan, the only thing the upgrade changes is Payhip’s own fee. Here is the maths for a few monthly sales levels, ignoring processing fees:

Rule of thumb: Plus starts to beat the free plan at roughly $970 a month in sales, and Pro starts to beat Plus at roughly $3,500 a month. Until you are selling steadily, the free plan is the sensible place to start, because it only costs you when you make a sale.

The Honest Reality: Payhip Does Not Sell For You

This is the point most Payhip reviews gloss over. The platform is excellent at processing a transaction once someone has decided to buy. What it cannot do is bring that person to you in the first place.

That is not a criticism of Payhip specifically. It is true of every digital product platform that is not a marketplace like Etsy or Amazon. You are responsible for driving the audience, whether through content, search, social media or email. Payhip handles everything once they arrive.

“The platform works. The work is building the audience that uses it.”

Payhip vs Gumroad

Gumroad is the platform most people compare Payhip with. Both let you sell digital products without technical skills, and both take a percentage of sales on their entry-level options. The differences that matter are how the fees are structured, how much control you get over your storefront and whether you want to rely on a platform’s own discovery features. We use both: our Digital Revenue Studio shop sells through Payhip, and the Atker Consultancy tools are sold on Gumroad.

Our Verdict

Payhip is a solid, legitimate starting point if you want to sell digital products without technical overhead and without paying a monthly fee before you have proven demand. The free plan is genuinely usable, the checkout is clean and setup is quick. The limitation is the same as any non-marketplace platform: you have to bring your own traffic. If you have an audience, or a plan to build one, Payhip is a good foundation to sell from.
