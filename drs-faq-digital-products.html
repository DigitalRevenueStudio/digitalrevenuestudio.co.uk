Digital products

Questions Answered. The Honest Version.

Selling digital products sounds passive. It is not. These answers come from selling our own products and our client brands’ on Payhip and Gumroad: planning, platforms, pricing, refunds and tax, and why traffic matters more than the product.

Based on our own experience.

Planning a product
What should my first digital product be?+

Something small that solves one specific problem for people you can already reach. A checklist, a template, a short guide or a set of prompts is easier to finish, easier to explain and easier to price than a full course. Our own first products were reference guides and workbooks, and the lesson was that a narrow, well-organised product beats a big one that tries to cover everything.

How do I know if anyone wants it?+

Look for the questions people are already asking. Reddit threads, Facebook groups, Amazon reviews of similar products and the “People also ask” boxes in Google all show real demand. If you cannot find anyone asking about the problem, that is a warning sign. A free version, such as a short guide or a sample, is a cheap way to test interest before you build the paid one.

Should I give something away for free?+

Usually, yes. A free product or tool gives people a reason to trust you before they buy. Our free Prompt Studio and the free Whiteout Survival wiki tools do that job: they are useful on their own, and they show the quality of the paid products without any pressure. A free download that needs an email address can also start an email list, which you own in a way you never own a social media audience.

The platforms
What is the difference between Payhip and Gumroad?+

Payhip is UK-based. Its free plan takes 5% of each sale, and paid plans lower that fee in exchange for a monthly price. Payhip says it collects and pays EU and UK VAT on digital products for you, which takes a real burden off a small seller. It is a checkout and delivery service, not a marketplace, so it will not send you shoppers.

Gumroad is US-based. It takes a percentage plus a small fixed fee on each sale, with a higher cut on sales that come through its Discover marketplace. Fees change, so check each platform’s pricing page before you choose. For the full Payhip breakdown with worked examples, read our Payhip review for UK sellers.

Why do you use Payhip for some brands and Gumroad for others?+

The Digital Revenue Studio shop and Fathom Guides sell through Payhip, because it handles UK and EU VAT, suits UK sellers and lets us run memberships. The Atker Consultancy construction tools are sold through Gumroad. Both work. The platform matters far less than where your buyers come from.

Does it cost anything to list products?+

No. Listing is free on both platforms on their entry-level options, and they take their fee when you make a sale. Payment processing fees from Stripe or PayPal are charged on top, so the platform fee is never the whole cost of a sale.

What types of files can you sell?+

Both platforms support PDFs, ePubs, ZIP files, video, audio and software. For guides, workbooks and checklists, PDF is the natural format because anyone can open it. For templates, a ZIP of editable files (Word, Excel, Canva links) is often more useful to the buyer than a PDF they cannot change.

The reality of sales
Why did your first products not sell?+

Because nobody could find them. In our early months, our first products had 48 views across Payhip and Gumroad and no sales. The products were not the problem. With so few visitors, even a good conversion rate would have produced nothing.

The key lesson: digital products are not passive income until you have an audience. Without traffic, a well-made product earns nothing. Building the audience comes first.

That is why most of our work since has gone into things people actually search for and share: useful guides, free tools, Reddit answers and Pinterest, all pointing back to the products.

Do the platforms bring their own traffic?+

Not in a way you can rely on. Payhip is not a marketplace at all. Gumroad has a Discover section, but new products from unknown sellers get very little attention there. Think of the platform as your checkout and delivery system, not your shop window. You bring the visitors; the platform handles the payment and the download.

What price should you set for a digital product?+

Price against the value of the problem you solve, and against what similar products cost. A £1.99 product can feel throwaway, while £15 or more needs to feel clearly worth it. For practical guides and templates, a range of roughly £5 to £15 is common.

Do not cut your price because of low sales until you have real traffic. If nobody is visiting the page, price is not the problem yet.

Do bundles and memberships work?+

They can, once people already buy single products. A bundle gives existing buyers a reason to spend a little more. A membership has to give people a reason to stay every month, such as fresh content on a schedule, so it needs a steady supply of new material and an audience that trusts you. We would not start with either.

Creating the products
How do you make a digital product worth buying?+

Solve a specific problem that people are already searching for. Generic content does not convert. Specific, well-organised, immediately usable content does.

The effort is in the curation and organisation, not just the collection. A guide organised by use case beats a dump of everything you know.
What tools did you use to create the products?+

Our first products were laid out in Microsoft Word and PowerPoint and exported to PDF. No specialist design software was needed. Since then we have also used design tools and AI tools for research, drafting and layout, but every product is planned, checked and edited by us before it goes on sale.

Can I sell products made with AI?+

You can, but be careful. Check the rules of the platform you sell on, and be honest with buyers about what they are getting. AI can speed up research, drafting and design, but a product that is just unchecked AI output rarely solves anyone’s problem well, and it can contain mistakes you are responsible for. The value is in your knowledge, your checking and how well it is organised.

What stops people sharing my files?+

Not much, honestly. Platforms can limit download attempts and add the buyer’s email to PDFs, which discourages casual sharing. The better protection is to keep improving the product and give buyers a reason to come back for the updated version.

Rules, refunds and tax
Do I have to offer refunds on digital products?+

In the UK, customers usually have 14 days to cancel an online purchase. For digital content, that right can end once the download starts, but only if the buyer agreed to immediate access and acknowledged they would lose the right to cancel. Faulty products must still be put right. Set out your policy clearly before people buy. You can see how we handle it in our refund policy. This is general information, not legal advice.

Do you need to handle VAT on digital products?+

VAT on digital products sold to EU customers applies from the first sale, but Payhip says it collects and pays EU and UK VAT for you, and other platforms have their own arrangements, so check yours. Separately, you only need to register for UK VAT yourself if your taxable turnover goes over the VAT threshold, which is £90,000 at the time of writing.

Do I need to tell HMRC about my sales?+

Usually, if your income from selling is more than the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year. Above that, you will normally need to register for Self Assessment and report it. Check GOV.UK for the current rules, or speak to an accountant if you are unsure. This is general information, not tax advice.
